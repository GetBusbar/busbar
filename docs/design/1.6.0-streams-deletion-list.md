# 1.6.0 streams — what the generic duplex mount replaces, and what blocks each deletion

The duplex seam is a FACE. `busbar_contract_transport::session::DuplexWire` is "a wire that
upgrades and pumps": one call waits and hands back an open session as a value, the other takes that
value and runs the session to its end. `busbar-transport-ws` implements it, and its real-socket
session battery drives the two halves through it.

**The composition root's side has now landed WHOLE.** `root::plane_mount` — one acceptor, generic
over the face, mounting any plane's declared duplex surface and draining it — and `root::session_driver`
— the one thing it hands each open session to, generic over the kernel's units, reading the plane's
session behaviour off the plane's own registered row — are both on the tree behind `root-duplex-serve`.
Neither names a plane.

What has still NOT happened is any deletion, and the reason has MOVED rather than gone. It is no
longer "there is no `SessionDriver` to hand the acceptor"; there is one. It is that the driver does
not yet call the plane, because there is no per-unit arena that ships — and every remaining entry
below turns out to be waiting on the plane call rather than on the serving path. This file is the
list, with the exact blocker on each entry, so the next landing starts from a statement rather than
from a re-derivation.

## 1. The generic acceptor itself (`root::plane_mount`, behind `root-duplex-serve`) — LANDED

**State.** Landed. Generic over `W: DuplexWire`, so the module names no wire's type, no wire's crate,
no protocol and no plane; seven cells green against a made-up plane; the wire taken off the
composition root's seal (`registry::seal(..).transports.ws`) rather than constructed, with the
seventh cell asking by IDENTITY — the registry's entry for the key and the wire the acceptor mounts
are the same allocation — so the wire stays registered in exactly one place and
`kind-isolation:transport-registration` is green on it. The switch carries `duplex-wire`, the wire's
own condition, and no plane's name; it is default-off, so `duplex-ws-default-edge` still reads a
`--no-default-features` graph without the WS crate and `scripts/plane-delete-test.sh voice` still
passes with the acceptor composed.

**What unblocked it: the declared raise, on the cell rather than on the crate.** The blocker was
`kind-isolation:matrix`, an EXACT ratchet — every crate × kind cell must EQUAL today's measurement,
a count above its row is a landing that grew the coupling and a count below it is stale slack — and
a new module in the composition root cannot land at an unchanged count. The route through is the one
the ratchet already had and had never been used for a cell: `construction:ceiling-rose` reads
`[gate.ceiling_raises]` in `qa/construction.toml`, and an entry names the file, the exact dotted path
and BOTH numbers, carries a reason, and expires by itself — once the commit lands the base carries
the new number, the rise is gone, and the entry describes nothing and is refused as stale.

Three cells were declared, all of them `busbar`, and the numbers are measured rather than predicted:

| cell | from | to | where the rise comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `busbar × contract-transport` | 18 | 28 | the seam the acceptor is written against — `DuplexWire`, `SessionBudgets`, `SessionDriver`, `SessionEnd` — spelled through the contract rather than through the wire's crate, precisely so no transport type is named at the root |
| `busbar × kernel` | 158 | 159 | one hit: the battery asking the registry, in the kernel's own vocabulary, whether the wire it mounts is the entry the boot seal registered |
| `busbar × transport` | 939 | 970 | the root's own vocabulary — the registry the wire is taken off, the listener the node already bound, the manifest switch that says this build carries a duplex wire |

`busbar × plane` FELL, 1798 → 1772, and was re-pinned because `ceiling-slack` requires a ceiling to
equal its measurement. A fall is not a raise and is not declared.

**Why `busbar` may declare and nobody else may.** The composition root is a WIRE-PERMITTED kind: it
is the one place in the tree allowed to name what it composes, and every one of these hits is the
root naming the wire it mounts. A rise in a non-permitted crate is coupling and is drained instead —
which is what being generic over the face buys, since it is the reason no such rise happened.

**The two routes NOT taken**, recorded because the previous cut of this work proposed the first of
them. Promoting `DuplexWire` and its three companions to `busbar_contract`'s ROOT re-export would
have closed the `contract-transport` half exactly (28 → 18) by moving where the name is spelled — a
count hidden rather than a coupling drained. And a hand-edit of a `[[cell]]` count with no
declaration is the ratchet's own bypass, one line away, in the file it guards.

## 1b. The session driver (`root::session_driver`, behind `root-duplex-serve`) — LANDED

**State.** Landed. `SessionLoopDriver` is what `serve_until` hands each open session to: generic over
the kernel's `Units`, handed a declared `WireSurface` it cannot ask the provenance of, and with no
plane, dialect, protocol or wire named in the file. Fourteen cells, every one of them driving a
surface no plane in this tree declares over units that are nobody's. The bar is resolved at the open
before anything is allocated; the ordinal is checked rather than assumed; `close` removes under the
table's lock so "exactly once" is enforced rather than requested; and each frame's unit carries THAT
session's identity on its `UnitCtx`, which is what links a session's postings and its audit records
to the session rather than to nobody.

**THERE IS EXACTLY ONE SESSION SHAPE, and it is the contract's own boot check that says so.** The
first cut carried two — a run and a single answer — read off the declaration's `Answering` word. The
battery found it dead: `check_surface` refuses a duplex row declared `Unary` (`DuplexNotStreaming`)
before any listener binds, because a session whose first answer is its last is a session cut at its
first answer. So every mountable duplex binding in this tree declares a run, one inbound frame is one
unit, and `declares_a_run` reads the word as the CONDITION for serving a binding rather than as a
switch between two behaviours. Recorded here because it is a fact about the declaration vocabulary
and not about this file: a future duplex plane cannot declare its way into the other shape.

**What the plane's row supplies, and what it does not yet.** `busbar-plane-streams` needed no change.
Its `SURFACE` already carries everything the driver reads — two bindings, their mounts, `Bar::Credential`
on both, `Answering::Stream`, and `MEDIA_JSON` for the frames — and the driver reads it as data
through the same face the mount does. What the driver does not yet read is the media type, and that
is not an oversight: it writes NO frames, so there is nothing to put one on.

**WHAT THE DRIVER STILL DOES NOT DO, and it is the same two things `LoopDriver` states.** It does not
call the plane. There is no per-unit arena that ships, so there is nothing to build a plane `Ctx`
around, and `ProductionUnits` answers every non-admin step with a refusal. The previous cut of this
work (`33bccd28c`) did call the plane — through `root/plane_ctx.rs`'s `with_session_frame` and
`ArrivalTransport`, over the ARENA chain (`c0d7670ad`/`a5ff804ea`/`d2defbe2a`/`4e1aa4650`/`6c8f963d0`)
that is not on this tip and is not landing. Reproducing that call over machinery that does not ship
would be wiring it half-built and calling it served. So the loop really runs, the ending really is
the loop's, the session identity really is on the unit, and the frame back is EMPTY.

**Raises: five, all `busbar`.** caps 302 -> 319, contract-transport 28 -> 38, control 1063 -> 1066,
kernel 159 -> 174, transport 970 -> 980, measured rather than predicted. Three of them EXTEND the
acceptor's own standing declarations rather than adding a second entry against the same ceiling —
two declarations of one ceiling are two opinions about it, and the one that expired first would
leave the other describing a rise that had already happened. Nothing outside `busbar` moved by a
line, which is what being generic over both the wire and the units buys.

**One shared seam, one line.** `outcome_of` in `root/transports.rs` — THE ONE MAPPING from a loop
ending into the eight words — has its gate widened from `root-admin` to the union of its two readers.
A build carrying the acceptor without the admin surface would otherwise need a second copy, at which
point the two credential doors would be kept apart in one file and, one day, collapsed in the other.

## 2. `compose_voice_governed_calls` (`crates/busbar/src/main.rs`)

**Still blocked, and the blocker is now NAMED PRECISELY rather than named as "the session driver".**
This is the composition-root write of the plane's governed-call port: the moment a served session
becomes a governed one. With the acceptor AND the driver landed, the accept-and-drive shape has
exactly one answer in the tree — `plane_mount::serve_until` over `session_driver::SessionLoopDriver`
— and any second answer is a duplicate rather than a peer.

**But this glue is not an accept-and-drive shape and never was, and that is the correction this
landing makes to the previous reading of the entry.** What `compose_voice_governed_calls` writes is
`busbar_voice::mount::install_governed_calls`: the table a client-served tool call's reply reaches
the unit that is waiting on it. The acceptor does not subsume that and the driver does not subsume
that. What subsumes it is the frame reaching the plane's OWN units — at which point the wait is
entered where the leg is planned, on the real node, and the table is the real node's.

So the blocker is the plane call, and the plane call's blocker is the arena. The function's own
header already says the rest of it: the stub node it builds carries "the root's unbound posture" —
no groups, a flat pricer, an unbound auth chain, a memory journal — and says in as many words that
the posture "is honest for exactly as long as this node serves no unit". The moment a frame really
routes through the plane's units, that posture becomes WRONG rather than merely unused, and the
commit that lands the plane call is the one that has to thread the deployment's real auth, rate cards
and data directory in. Deleting this glue before then would take the governed-call table out of a
node that still has no other one; deleting it after is the same commit's own cleanup.

**Landing preconditions, in order:** (1) an arena that ships, so a plane `Ctx` can be built; (2) the
driver's plane call — `decode_ingress` / the loop / `encode_refusal`, with `PlaneSessionState` on the
slot and the declared media on the reply; (3) the real deployment parts threaded into the node the
plane's units run on. This entry is deletable in (3) and not before.

## 1c. The plane call (Track 1 step 5) — LANDED, and what it moved

**State.** `SessionLoopDriver` calls the plane. Per moment of a session an `ArenaSpace` goes on the
driver's own stack (A1's shipping `root::arena`), a `UnitArena` is carved from it, the contract
`Ctx` is built on that, and:

* `open` runs THE OPENING UNIT at the upgrade — the one moment a refusal is answerable on the
  wire — and its ending is the upgrade's answer; the plane's client half (`open_session`) is
  opened on the same stack and is the slot's only if the unit completed;
* `drive` hands the frame to `decode_ingress` against the slot's codec state, runs a draft as a
  unit of the loop under the session's identity, and renders a refusal through the plane's own
  `encode_refusal` under the media the row declares (`declared_run`); a frame the plane cannot
  read is answered the same way and runs no unit; `NeedMore`/`Frame`/`Close`/`Discard` are
  consumed quietly, with no media on the reply;
* `open_upstream` is called once per session, on the arena, the moment the composed units say
  where the leg was sealed to, and the state is held on the slot.

**Session units are composed as DATA.** `root::session_driver::SessionUnits` — one unit per
moment, borrowed from the composition for the moment; where the leg was sealed to; the session is
over. `ProductionUnits` carries `duplex: Option<Arc<dyn SessionUnits>>` (`with_duplex_units`) and
implements the seam over it, falling back to its own unclaimed refusal. That is the composition
gate A1 named, closed from the honest side, and no plane is named on the shared struct. The voice
plane's composition is `root::units_voice::ComposedUnits` over an `Arc<VoiceNode>`: unit zero's
Verify settles the session on the node (dialect, chain, the leg sealed in the plane's shape), every
later unit reads the settlement, `closed` unbinds it and the open calls with it.

**What it does NOT do, so nobody looks in the wrong place.** The RELAY (`Ingress::Frame` onto the
open turn, `encode_ingress_frame`) and the turn's CLOSE are the upstream half's: they need the
open unit's `Unit` view and the socket the leg is written on, and neither is on the driver.
`encode_egress` / `encode_end` take a contract `Unit`, which only the kernel seal can build and
the seal is banned outside the kernel — so "open_upstream dials from a real EgressBody" is met as
far as `open_upstream(dest, ctx)` on the arena and NOT as far as the body: the body needs a unit
view the kernel does not yet hand out. That is the next seam, and it is a kernel one.

**Entry 2 (`compose_voice_governed_calls`) is NOT yet deletable, and the reason moved again.** It
is no longer "the plane call". It is that `main.rs` composes NO driver: `root-duplex-serve` is
default-off and nothing in `run()` mounts a driver at all. `install_governed_calls` is therefore
still the only path by which a served voice session is a governed one, and deleting it would
un-govern the shipped path.

**CORRECTED BELOW.** This entry said the mount was `serve_until` over `SessionLoopDriver` in
`run()`. It is not, and it cannot be: see §1d for the mount that fits (a root-owned `WsArrivalSpec`
over `busbar_transport_ws::mount::{open_session, pump}`), §1e for why the deletion is one blocker
for all six names rather than one per name, and §1f for the kernel seam that unblocks it and the
condition on which it is owed.

## What is now deletable in `busbar-voice` (the 7.9k-line body), and what it waits on

**With the plane call landed, the CLIENT half of the body is subsumed on the root's side and the
UPSTREAM half is not.** Subsumed: the per-frame client read (`decode_ingress` over the slot's
state), the per-unit judgement (this plane's own `Units`, under the session's identity), the
refusal rendering, the session-open unit and its audit record, the open-call table's `closed`
(the composition's `closed` unbinds the session's calls). Still owed by the body: the provider
socket and the relay onto it, the tool moat's in-process executor, and the `GovernedCalls` wake
from a client reply (the composition can read a `Frame`'s correlation, but the wake is the
upstream half's frame path). So `busbar_voice::runtime::{SessionCore, UplinkForwarder}`'s
client-read half and `mount::install_governed_calls`'s client half become deletable in the SEAM
commit that mounts the driver in `main.rs`; the pump's upstream half and `ToolExecutor` stay
until the relay lands.

**The previous statement, kept for the record:** Nothing yet, and the reason is worth stating rather than re-derived next time. The body is
reached from the composition root through exactly one write — `busbar_voice::mount::install_governed_calls`,
from `compose_voice_governed_calls` — and through `busbar_voice::runtime`'s `GovernedCalls` and
`ToolExecutor` seams, which are the I/O half of a live session: the pump that owns the socket to the
provider, the tool moat that executes an in-process tool and authors the `function_call_output`
itself, and the runtime that accumulates streamed arguments across frames.

The driver that landed here serves the CLIENT side of a session: it takes the upgrade, holds the
per-session bookkeeping, and runs each inbound frame as a unit. It dials no upstream, opens no
provider session and executes no tool, and it holds nothing that could. `plane-delete-test voice`
passes with the acceptor and the driver composed precisely because neither reaches the body at all.

So the body's deletion waits, in order, on:

1. **the plane call** (entry 2's precondition 2), which is what makes a frame mean anything at all
   on the root's side;
2. **the upstream half**, which is what `busbar-plane-streams`'s `open_upstream` declares and nothing
   in the root yet dials: until a served session can reach a provider, deleting the runtime that does
   would delete the only working path;
3. **the tool moat**, which is the part of the body with no declared seam on the root's side yet —
   `ToolExecutor` runs a tool in-process and authors the result, and the governed table is only the
   half that says a CLIENT answered. The root has the second half and not the first.

None of those three is blocked on the ratchet, the wire, the registration, the drain or the driver
any more. All three are blocked on the arena.

## 1d. THE MOUNT — where it actually goes, and why `serve_until` is not it

**State: NOT mounted, and the shape the previous entries assumed is the wrong one.** This section
replaces "mount `serve_until` over `SessionLoopDriver` in `main.rs`" wherever the entries above
imply it. That call cannot be the mount, and the reason is a fact about this tree rather than a
preference.

**A served voice session on this tree never touches a contract `Listener`.** `main.rs` binds
`tokio::net::TcpListener` and serves an axum router (`serve_listener`, `serve_thread_per_core`);
`root::transports::listen_all` — the one thing that produces a `busbar_contract::wire::Listener` —
is called from nowhere in `main.rs`. The voice sessions arrive as ROUTES on that router:
`register_ws_arrivals` installs `busbar_voice::mount::voice_ws_arrivals()` into the substrate's
WS-accept registry, `busbar_core::router` drains it (`take_ws_arrivals`) and mounts one gauntlet-gated
route per spec, and each one calls `busbar_voice::mount::ws_accept`, which reaches
`busbar_substrate::ingress::duplex_ws::accept_gauntlet` and gets the upgraded socket as a
`(Receiver<Vec<u8>>, UnboundedSender<Vec<u8>>)` pair.

`plane_mount::serve_until` takes a `Listener` and does its own tungstenite handshake. Handing it
the node's socket is not possible (the node has no `Listener`), and binding it one is the second
address for one protocol that `plane_mount`'s own header refuses. So the acceptor is not the seam
the shipped path is missing; it is the seam a `Listener`-served deployment would use, and this
deployment is not one.

**THE MOUNT THAT DOES FIT, and every part of it already exists and is reachable.**
`busbar-transport-ws` exports `pub mod mount`, and the two calls inside it that the acceptor uses are
BOTH generic over the socket rather than over the wire:

* `mount::address(surface, key, chain, &Upgrade)` + `mount::open_session(driver, surface, &mount, &upgrade)`
  — address an arriving upgrade against the plane's declared `SURFACE` and open a session ON THE
  DRIVER, before the protocol changes, so a refusal is still answerable as a status. The `Upgrade`
  it takes is `{ target, peer, credential }` — three fields the `WsArrival` already carries verbatim
  (`uri`, the peer, the `Authorization` header).
* `mount::pump(driver, session, source, sink, budgets)` — generic over `FrameSource`/`FrameSink`,
  NOT over `DuplexWire`. The substrate channel pair satisfies both with an adapter the composition
  writes; nothing in the pump wants a tungstenite socket.

So the mount is a root-owned `WsArrivalSpec` whose accept fn runs the plane's gauntlet, accepts the
upgrade through the substrate seam, and drives the resulting channel through
`mount::open_session` + `mount::pump` over `SessionLoopDriver` — no second listener, no
`serve_until`, and no plane named in the acceptor (the specs are built from the plane's declared
`SURFACE`, exactly as the driver reads its behaviour off the same row).

## 1e. WHY THE DELETIONS ARE STILL BLOCKED, and it is ONE blocker for all of them

The blocker is no longer the mount and is not the arena. It is that **the driver owns no upstream
socket**, and two of the three served legs are nothing but that socket.

`ws_accept` has three arms and they are not the same shape:

| leg | what it does | can the driver serve it today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Sideband` | control-only; media is peer-to-peer by design, so there is no provider leg to dial | YES |
| `Telephony` | `TelephonyProxy::run` over a real dialled provider socket: client→provider relay both ways | NO |
| `Gemini` | the same proxy shape under the Gemini codec | NO |

The driver's `drive` reads the frame, runs the unit and renders the plane's refusal. It cannot
forward the frame upstream, because the frame's onward write is `encode_ingress_frame` onto a socket
`dial_provider` opened and `TelephonyProxy` owns — and the driver holds neither. So mounting the
driver on `Telephony`/`Gemini` would take the relay out of the only path that has one, and the
conformance battery's provider-dial, route-failover and cross-parity legs would be measuring a node
that no longer relays.

And mounting it on `Sideband` ALONE deletes nothing, which is the second half of the finding:
`VoiceSession` (the per-frame client read) and `UplinkForwarder` are both held by
`topology::telephony` as the proxy's downlink and uplink planes (`telephony.rs`, two construction
sites each), and `open_admitted_session` is reached from the no-provider fallback as well as from
`Sideband`. A sideband-only mount leaves every one of them live, so it is a second serving path
beside the one that ships — build-beside-legacy, with no deletion to pay for it.

`compose_voice_governed_calls` and `install_governed_calls` follow the same rule they always did:
they may go on the commit where EVERY served session is the composition's, and that is the commit
where the relay lands, not before. Deleting them while telephony still opens its own sessions
un-governs the shipped path.

## 1f. THE NEXT SEAM, named precisely — and it is a kernel one

`Plane::encode_egress` and `Plane::encode_end` — the two writes of a unit's upstream side, which the
relay needs — take a `busbar_contract::unit::Unit<'u>`. `Unit::new` takes a
`&dyn busbar_contract::plugin::KernelSeal`, and **nothing in this tree calls it**: a workspace-wide
search for `Unit::new` finds only `AdmissionUnit::new` / `VoiceUnit::new`, so the contract `Unit`
is a type the production code has never constructed and those two encoders have never run outside a
plane's own tests.

The seam is therefore on `busbar_kernel::teller::Kernel`, beside `admit_token` /
`transport_key_token` / `admin_token` / `ledger_token` — the named mints that exist precisely so the
source scan can account for every use of the seal. What is missing is the same shape one step over:
a unit VIEW, minted from a draft the loop has already run plus the identity only the kernel seals
(key, origin, session, stream, direction, principal), handed to the composition for the length of
one call. Without it `encode_egress` has a parameter no caller outside the kernel can supply, which
is exactly the reason `transport_key_token` and `admin_token` were added for their own units.

**But it is not owed YET, and the condition is worth stating so it is not landed early.** The driver
has nowhere to write an `EgressBody`: it holds no upstream socket. A commit that minted the view and
called `encode_egress` would produce bytes nothing sends. So the order is: the upstream socket first
(who dials it, who owns it across the session, and where it hangs off the driver's slot), the kernel
unit-view seam with it in the same landing, and the deletions on the far side of both.

**The order, restated as one list.** (1) the root-owned `WsArrivalSpec` mount of §1d, with the
channel adapter for `mount::pump`; (2) the upstream socket on the driver's slot, and the
`Kernel::unit_view` seam that lets `encode_egress` / `encode_ingress_frame` / `encode_end` be called
against it; (3) the three legs move onto the driver together, and `VoiceSession`, `UplinkForwarder`,
`open_admitted_session`, `TelephonyProxy`'s two planes, `compose_voice_governed_calls` and
`install_governed_calls` all go in that one commit, because they are one path and not six.

## 1g. WHERE THE LINE IS AFTER THE LEG — measured on `keep-voice-mount-seam` dd5fa3045

Steps (1) and (2) of the order above are now split differently from how §1f guessed, and the split
is worth recording because it changes what commit (3) still owes.

**Closed since §1f.** The upstream socket is on the slot and the kernel's unit view exists, so the
three encoders can be called. Both directions of a relayed session now run: `Ingress::Frame` is a
WRITE on the leg under the open unit's own view, `Ingress::Close` settles it and finishes the lease,
and the leg's INBOUND half is read by `root::leg_pump::pump_leg` — the plane's `decode_response`
against the leg's codec state, `encode_response` against the client's, out on the client's own
offering end, with a reply that cannot be written ending the session in the same two words the relay
ends it in. `UnitIdentity.principal` is no longer `None`: `SessionUnits::principal` publishes what
the composition's own authenticate step resolved, off the same settlement the destination comes off.
The dial is told which session it is for, because the composition — not the decorator — is the party
that can pump a leg for the life of a session.

**Still owed by the mount, and now named precisely.** Three things block `root/ws_arrival.rs`, and
two of them are faces that do not exist rather than work that has not been done.

1. **The two operator-hook calls have no neutral face.** `ws_accept` runs the hooks-gate
   (`mount.rs` `hook_gate`) and the hooks-tap (`hook_tap`) BEFORE the upgrade, and both serialize a
   `busbar_voice_codec::ir::config::SessionConfig` — a PLANE's type, in a plane-kind crate — over
   `EngineHost::gate_decide` / `transform_over`. A root that carried those calls would name that
   type, which is the composition root naming a plane's payload shape. Neither call is a unit step
   and the root has no hook seam on the session path, so what is missing is a face for "the declared
   session parameters a gate or a tap sees", neutral over planes, in the same shape the one-shot
   path's gate seam has. ~~That face is UNDESIGNED.~~ **CLOSED, 1.6.0.**
   `busbar_contract::plane::SessionParams` + `SessionPlane::{session_params, adopt_session_params}`
   is that face: a PROJECTOR pair on the plane's own row, not a re-expression. The hook wire already
   carries the plane's session parameters as OPAQUE BYTES (`GateSubjectRef.args`) beside the neutral
   envelope, so a re-expressed payload would have changed the argument bytes every deployed

## 1h. WHAT THE SEAM COMMIT STILL OWES AFTER §1g's THREE — measured on `da2a74e4a`

§1g's three were the blockers as they read from the leg. Read from the MOUNT — with the arrival
file's own composition written out rather than assumed — three of them are closed (items 1 and 3 by
their own landings, item 2 by the entry above) and **five more are open that §1g does not name**.
None of them is a re-derivation: each is a grep or a signature on this tip. They are recorded here
because §1g currently reads as "one design slot away", and it is not.

**Every one of them is a blocker on the SAME commit**, which is the point §1e makes and this entry
does not weaken: the three legs move together, so a leg that cannot be served blocks the deletion of
all six names, not of one.

1. **THE ROOT COMPOSES THE PLANE WITH NO UPSTREAMS, so no session on the driver can ever seal a
   destination.** `main.rs:860` is `busbar_plane_streams::VoicePlane::new(&[])` — the empty list —
   and its own comment says why: *"none today — the `streams:` reader that fills it is the same work

### 2a. `install_governed_calls` IS ALREADY GONE — six references above are STALE

`busbar_voice::mount::install_governed_calls` is named **six times** in the sections above

### 2b. THE THREE DIALECT CRATES ARE CUT, AND THEY DELETED NOTHING FROM `busbar-voice`

All three of the streams plane's dialects are now crates of their own —
`busbar-plane-streams-twilio` (VT-2), `busbar-plane-streams-gemini` and
`busbar-plane-streams-openai` (ST-4) — registered by the composition root, each removable by
`scripts/plane-delete-test.sh`. What they deleted is in `busbar-plane-streams`, the NEUTRAL plane:
three `&'static Dialect` rows, three name constants, an empty `DECLARED` table, two
`if dialect.name == <one vendor>` bodies that picked a codec, and five call sites that answered
"no dialect was negotiated" with one particular vendor's row by name.

**They deleted nothing from `busbar-voice`, and that is the honest measurement rather than an
omission.** The reason is §1e's, unchanged and now sharper:

* the **openai-realtime** leg IS `busbar-voice`'s spine. `Ingress::Sideband` and
  `Ingress::Telephony` both run on it, the one-shot `Mint` and `Sdp` HTTP passes share its
  `open_governed` / `finish` / `TurnMeter` path, and `topology/webrtc.rs` and
  `topology/minter_https.rs` exist for those passes. Deleting "the openai-realtime body" is deleting
  the crate, and the crate still serves the one leg that serves anything (VT-7's
  `sideband_serves_a_session`);
* the **gemini-live** leg is separable — `Ingress::Gemini`, `GEMINI_PATH`, `GEMINI_MOUNT_PATH`,
  `GEMINI_API_KEY_HEADER`, `VoiceMount::gemini_provider`, `provider_ws_url`'s query-string branch,
  `redact_url_credentials` (which exists ONLY because that vendor puts the key in the query string),
  and `crate::GEMINI_LIVE` are roughly 250 production lines that touch nothing else. But deleting
  them **unserves `/v1/realtime/gemini/{call_id}` with nothing to serve it instead**: the plane
  still CLAIMS that URL, `root-duplex-serve` is default-off, and the generic driver cannot serve
  this leg for §1e's own reason (it owns no upstream socket, and this leg is nothing but one). A
  deletion that turns a served URL into a 404 while its claim stands is not a deletion, it is a
  regression with a smaller diff.

**THE ONE LINE THAT KILLS BOTH**: the commit §1f/§1g name — the three legs moving onto the driver
TOGETHER, once the driver owns the upstream socket. On that commit `Ingress` loses all three of its
duplex variants at once, `ws_accept` goes with them, `VoiceSession`, `UplinkForwarder`,
`open_admitted_session`, `TelephonyProxy`'s two planes and `compose_voice_governed_calls` go in the
same commit for the reason §1f already gives (they are one path and not six), and the dialect
crates are what the driver reads the wire through. The dialect cuts are the half of that commit that
could land early, because a row moving out of a neutral crate serves no byte and un-serves none.

**Two smaller things the cuts DID retire, measured.** The third spelling of both dialect names —
`busbar_voice::{OPENAI_REALTIME, GEMINI_LIVE}` are `"openai_realtime"` and `"gemini_live"`, with
UNDERSCORES, a 1.5.5 wire-format key that is neither the plane's `"openai-realtime"` nor the
dialect crate's `NAME` — is now the ONLY place either underscore spelling appears, so the crate
that dies takes it with it. And `busbar_voice_codec::topology::twilio` (391 lines + 270 of cells),
the duplicate carrier envelope VT-2 named as "dying with the legacy line", is still live and still
behind a `runtime` feature nothing turns on.
