# 1.6.0 streams — what the generic duplex mount replaces, and what blocks each deletion

The duplex seam is now a FACE. `busbar_contract_transport::session::DuplexWire` is "a wire that
upgrades and pumps": one call waits and hands back an open session as a value, the other takes that
value and runs the session to its end. `busbar-transport-ws` implements it, and its real-socket
session battery drives the two halves through it.

What is NOT yet landed is the composition root's side — the one generic acceptor
(`root::plane_mount`) that mounts any plane's declared duplex surface and drains it — and therefore
none of the plane-named serving glue the acceptor is meant to replace has been deleted. This file is
the list, with the exact blocker on each entry, so the next landing starts from a statement rather
than from a re-derivation.

## 1. The generic acceptor itself (`root::plane_mount`, behind `root-duplex-serve`)

**State.** Written, generic over `W: DuplexWire`, six cells green against a made-up plane, the wire
taken off the composition root's seal (`registry::seal(..).transports.ws`) rather than constructed —
so the wire stays registered in exactly one place and
`cargo xtask gate kind-isolation:transport-registration` is green on it, which is the row that
parked the previous cut of this work.

**Blocker: `kind-isolation:matrix`, and it is an EXACT ratchet.** Every crate × kind cell in
`qa/kind-isolation.toml` must EQUAL today's measurement — a count above its row is a landing that
grew the coupling and a count below it is stale slack, and both are red. A new module in the
composition root cannot land at an unchanged count:

| cell | pinned | with the acceptor | where the rise comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `busbar × contract-transport` | 18 | 30 | `busbar_contract::transport::…` reads as the segment run `busbar contract transport`: 4 in `plane_mount.rs`, 8 in its battery |
| `busbar × transport` | 938 | ~964 | the battery names the wire it takes off the registry (`transports.ws`, `busbar_transport_ws::StaticConfig`, the fixture's `transport: "ws"` binding key) and the module's prose names the listener's own protocol |

Neither is reducible to zero by rewriting, and that is the point rather than a complaint: the
composition root naming the wire it composed is what a composition root IS, and the acceptor's
battery has to bind a real listener on a real registered wire or it is proving something else. The
ratchet has no reviewed way to record a landing that legitimately adds vocabulary — `[[announced]]`
covers a CRATE being created, not a cell — so landing the acceptor needs one of:

* a route from the composition root to the duplex seam that does not spell
  `busbar_contract::transport::` — i.e. the four names it implements against
  (`DuplexWire`, `SessionBudgets`, `SessionDriver`, `SessionEnd`) promoted to `busbar_contract`'s
  ROOT re-export, beside `Transport`, `Listener` and `TransportError`, which the root already reaches
  that way. That closes the `contract-transport` half exactly (30 → 18) and is a contract surface
  change, so it is a `[SEAM]` commit of its own; **and**
* an owner decision on the `transport` half: either a reviewed raise of the `busbar × transport`
  cell with the acceptor named as its reason, or a `[[landing]]`-shaped row in the ledger that
  records a cell moving up with a citation, the way `[[announced]]` records a crate appearing.

Until then the acceptor is written and not landed. It is NOT parked for a design reason: nothing in
it is wrong, and no gate that judges its DESIGN is red.

## 2. `compose_voice_governed_calls` (`crates/busbar/src/main.rs`)

**Blocked on 1.** This is the composition-root write of the plane's governed-call port — the moment a
served session becomes a governed one. The generic acceptor is what serves the session; with no
acceptor landed there is nothing for this glue to be replaced BY, and deleting it would leave the
node unable to wake a client-served tool call's wait.

**Second blocker, independent of 1: the session-driver line.** The acceptor is handed a
`SessionDriver`, and the composition root has no production one for a plane yet — the driver that
turns a frame into a plane call over the arena chain is the work on `33bccd28c` and is not on this
tip. Until it is, `plane_mount::serve_until` has nothing but a scripted driver to be handed, so the
boot path cannot be moved onto it even with the ratchet resolved.

## 3. `root/units_voice.rs` boot glue the mount would serve

**Blocked on 2.** The parts of this file the mount subsumes are the ones reached only through
`compose_voice_governed_calls`; the rest is the plane's own units composition, which the mount does
not replace and must not. Splitting the two is a job for the commit that lands the session driver,
because that commit is what says which side of the line each piece is on. The plane's own crate keeps
its behaviour either way — nothing here is a deletion of the plane.

## 4. `root/voice_serve.rs`

**Not present on this base.** It was named in the work order; there is no such file at
`cd8b3e638`. Nothing to delete.

## What was landed instead

* the `DuplexWire` face and `SessionBudgets`, in `busbar-contract-transport::session`;
* `impl DuplexWire for WsTransport`, with the wire's own `SessionBudgets` declaration deleted and
  the seam's re-exported in its place, and the crate's measured reach into the transport contract
  down from 105 to 99;
* the wire's presence in the composition root spelled as the wire's own condition (`duplex-wire`)
  rather than as one plane's feature (`plane-voice`), which is the coupling entry 1 would otherwise
  have inherited: the registry composed, registered and dialled `ws` only when the VOICE plane was
  compiled. `busbar × plane` falls 1798 → 1769 and `busbar × transport` 939 → 938 with that one
  rename.
