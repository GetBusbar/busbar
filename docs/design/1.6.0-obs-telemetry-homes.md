### 1.1 A correction to CORE-HOMES §2.4: the guard suite is already a FOURTH copy

CORE-HOMES priced the guard suite as a move into `busbar_substrate::net_guard` on the strength of
two comments that name `observability::host_is_internal` as the original. Measured, the substrate's
`net_guard.rs` is **1,336 lines** and already holds the whole predicate family —
`ipv4_is_internal :104`, `ipv6_is_internal :148`, `ip_is_cloud_metadata :178`, `ip_is_internal :206`,
`dns_name_is_internal :219`, `is_alternate_ipv4_encoding :259`, `scheme_is :864`,
`host_is_private_or_loopback :1007`, `expand_alternate_ipv4 :1118`, `ssrf_blocked_host :1238`.
Core's `observability.rs` already CALLS three of them (`:15-17`). What is left in core is the two
**policy** functions (`validate_webhook_url :214` / `validate_otlp_endpoint :515`, which differ
deliberately on the `localhost` family and on `http://` to loopback), the two URL-shaped **atoms**
the policies need (`host_is_internal :283`, `is_internal_v4 :254`, `scheme_is :180`,
`is_alternate_loopback_v4 :729`, the `otlp_*` quartet `:588/:610/:625/:640/:673`), and the
credential handling (`mask_userinfo :37`, `base64_encode :76`, `split_otlp_credentials :117`).

So the honest transaction is not "move 450 lines". It is: **the sink POLICY goes to
`busbar-unit-egress`** (this is the unit that decides whether a destination may be dialled), the
duplicated range predicates **die against the substrate's** rather than travelling, and
`split_otlp_credentials`/`base64_encode` travel with the OTLP exporter, not with the guard —
they are OTLP auth-header construction, not an SSRF predicate.

## 3. Why not a `busbar-unit-observability`, and why the Tick is real

CORE-HOMES §2.4's reasoning holds and is not re-argued: telemetry emission is not a step (no
`Admit`, no token, no verdict), it fires from six places on the request path, and no `busbar-unit-*`
crate holds observability-shaped code. Making a unit for the EMIT spends `unit_total_ceiling` on
plumbing and puts a mandatory crate between the hot path and an atomic.

The DRAIN is the opposite shape and that is why exactly one thing here becomes a Tick. It is
periodic, it is not on the request path, it has a cadence derived from operator config, and it has
a correctness obligation on shutdown (the final drain) that the current detached thread cannot
meet. Everything else in the family is pull-driven off the scrape
(`busbar-substrate/src/telemetry.rs:507-515` from `metrics::render()`).

## 4. What landed, and what this note hands back

Items 2–6 landed. `busbar-core/src/observability.rs` is **deleted outright**: 819 production lines
at the start of this branch, zero at the end, and `pub mod observability;` with it. `legacy-reach`
fell 91 → 90 and its `busbar_core` sub-row 25 → 24, re-pinned downward rather than declared, which
is the owner's "SEQUENCE deletions first, no raise" arriving. Item 7 did not land, for a reason the
measurement found:

**The OTLP exporter is not an `Export`, and making it one is a redesign, not a move.** The
`busbar-export-otlp` `[[announced]]` row exists (EXPORT-1, `origin/keep-export-kind` 45ce4df09) and
its reason line reads *"the OTLP trace sink, the export half of `observability.rs`"*. But
`kind_isolation.rs` requires every crate of kind `export` to implement
`busbar_contract::kinds::Export` **exactly once** — `fn receive(&self, item: ExportItem<'u>) -> Ack`
— plus the `meta`/`export` skeleton (`kind_skeleton`) and a per-crate conformance battery
(`BATTERY_KINDS`). The body that left `observability.rs` receives **no `ExportItem` and never did**:
it is a `tracing_subscriber::Layer` built over a `hyper_rustls` connector and an
`SdkTracerProvider` batch pipeline, and spans reach it through the subscriber registry, not through
a sink call. Presenting it as an `Export` carrying the `Traces` stream means deciding how a span
becomes an `ExportItem` and how `Ack::{Durable, Received, Retry}` reads against a batch processor
that already has its own queue — which is §3's transaction (the projection grammar to
`busbar-contract`, `module_streams` at `export/projection.rs:132-140` deleted, the routes off
`plugin_routes.rs:271-284`), not a relocation.

So the body sits in the composition root, in `crates/busbar/src/root/logging.rs`, where boot code
belongs and where it costs no crate a ceiling, and it moves to `busbar-export-otlp` **in the commit
that decides the span-to-`ExportItem` question** — EXPORT's row, with the announce row struck there.
Nothing about that is harder for its having landed in the root first: the body is 90 lines in one
file with one caller.

**`refresh_scrape_gauges` + `emit_lane_gauges` (326 lines) — the explicit hand-back.** They stay in
`busbar-core/src/metrics.rs`. Their destination was the frozen substrate, and their one production
reader is `export/prometheus.rs:53`. Their home is **`busbar-export-prometheus`**, and they must
travel with that reader, re-typed off `&App` onto the neutral `EngineTablesView`
(`busbar-substrate/src/plane_host/engine_view.rs:57-91`) which they already read through; the
testkit face `refresh_scrape_gauges(&self)` (`busbar-substrate/src/testkit/mod.rs:90`) already
exists for them. They cannot move first: `refresh_scrape_gauges` runs INSIDE `render()`, so a
`busbar-export-prometheus` that serves `/metrics` without them serves stale gauges.

**The userinfo masker, for the webhook export sink.** `busbar_substrate::net_guard::redact_userinfo`
is private on a frozen crate. The face is now
**`busbar_unit_egress::sink_guard::mask_userinfo`** (`pub`), beside `validate_webhook_url` (`pub`),
which is the guard that sink's URL must pass anyway. `percent_decode` is `pub` there too.

**The `busbar-core → busbar-contract` edge.** Until it lands, core's `TelemetryHost` impl spells
the substrate alias. The edge is permitted (`kind_isolation.rs:534`); it costs one new
`busbar-core × contract` cell.
