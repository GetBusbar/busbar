# THE PLANE ABI, IN TWO LAYERS

**The rulings this is written to:**

* **R-N** — the protocol-plugin contract is **not locked**; nobody has consumed it. Design it for its
  purpose: hot-path protocol work. The store/secret/auth/hook/export ABI **is** frozen and this
  document does not touch it. Its cold-path JSON justification is correct *for its purpose* —
  `plugin-abi/src/lib.rs:12-21` says so in its own words, and the sentence that follows is the reason
  it does not transfer: *"the cost is irrelevant: these backends are off the request hot path (the
  store is write-behind, auth is cached)."*
* **R-O** — the design must generalise. VPN is the **stress test**, not a roadmap item.
* **R-K** — compiled-in vs dlopen is a packaging choice, not a capability limit.
* **The latency bar** — *"all plugins add nanoseconds not microseconds."*

# PART 0 — THE TWO RESULTS THAT REORGANISED THE DESIGN

The kickoff's proposal was: JSON is a parse and therefore linear in payload bytes, so adopt a
zero-copy format and the crossing becomes flat. **The first half is right and the second half is
false, and the measurement says so plainly.**

## 0.1 The read is bounded by the layer split — this part holds

A real FlatBuffers reader over a real flatc-generated schema, **reading** the fields core actually
needs (this measures the READ HALF of a crossing only — see §0.2, which is where an earlier draft of
this document went wrong):

| what is read | 629 B payload | 405,429 B payload | shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| whole IR, **unverified** (`root_as_chat_req_unchecked`) | ~13 ns | ~10 ns | **flat** — and **unsound** |
| whole IR, **verified** (`root_as_chat_req`) | ~722 ns | ~197,518 ns | **LINEAR** |
| Layer 1 facts only, **verified**, body untraversed | ~111 ns | **~75 ns** | **FLAT, and sound** |

*(Release profile; full table, spread and contention conditions in §4.3. The **shapes** are the
load-robust claim and they are what the argument rests on.)*

The reason is structural, not a property of FlatBuffers. A verifier at a trust boundary must walk
every string, table and table-vector it will later hand out as a typed reference — that walk is the
verification. It does **not** walk a scalar byte vector; it range-checks it once. So the cost of the
sound **read** is proportional to **how much of the payload core promises to have checked**, which is
proportional to **how much of the payload core reads**. Core reads the facts and not the conversation,
so the read is bounded.

## 0.2 THE CORRECTION — bounding the read is necessary and NOT sufficient

An earlier draft of this document stopped there and claimed a "Flatness Theorem" with *if and only
if* in it. **That was wrong, and the independent measurement branch
(`perf/1.6.0-plugin-abi-measurement`, `73de73c7`) is what caught it.** Fitting slopes over 10 points
across 2.5 decades, it found zero-copy is **not** flat end-to-end: `rkyv-prebuilt` costs **0.0366
ns/byte**, which tracks the pure-transport arm — allocate, `memcpy`, free — at **0.0301 ns/byte**
almost exactly.

**The two harnesses did not disagree. They measured different halves of the same crossing, and both
numbers are correct.** Stated exactly, because a contradiction left unresolved would have been built
on:

* **This document's PoC** builds the buffer *outside* the timing loop (`poc/plane-crossing/src/main.rs:348,350`)
  and times only `verify + read`. **No allocation, no `memcpy`, no free, no DSO transit** is inside
  the measured region. Its 75–112 ns is a true measurement of **the read half**, and mislabelling
  that column "ns per crossing" is the specific error.
* **The measurement branch's arm** includes the transport that the frozen contract mandates:
  *plugin allocates → host copies out → host calls `busbar_free`*. That copy is **O(bytes) by
  construction**, and it is entirely indifferent to how little of the buffer the host subsequently
  reads.

A bounded read set bounds the **parse and the verify**. It does nothing whatever to the **copy**.
Flat + linear = linear, so the crossing as currently contracted is linear no matter how good the
format is. Hence:

> **THE CROSSING THEOREM (corrected).** A sound plugin crossing is O(1) in payload bytes **if and
> only if BOTH**:
> 1. **the host's read set is bounded** independently of the payload — this bounds the *parse* and
>    the *verify*; and
> 2. **the transport borrows rather than copies** — this bounds the *transfer*.
>
> **Each condition is necessary; neither alone is sufficient.** (1) without (2) is memcpy-bound
> (~0.03 ns/B — a real 24x slope win over JSON's 0.733 ns/B, but still linear). (2) without (1) is
> parse-bound, and worse.

Condition (2) is a **transport-shape change**, and it must be proposed out loud rather than assumed
to arrive free with the archive format. It is legitimate here and only here: **R-N leaves the
protocol contract explicitly unlocked, and the store/auth/secret/hook/export ABI — where
copy-then-`busbar_free` stays exactly as it is — is frozen.** The borrow contract and its soundness
argument are §3.10.

**So the corrected headline is: the layer split buys the bounded read, borrow-not-copy buys the
bounded transfer, and the bar needs both.** The two-layer split and the `IrFacts`-not-`IrReq` move
remain the same proposal and the latency bar still independently forces them — that part survives.
What does not survive is the claim that they are sufficient on their own.

# PART 1 — THE TWO LAYERS

## 1.1 The split

**LAYER 1 — universal, IR-free.** Session lifecycle, duplex byte streams, the capability vtable,
admission, and the route/auth ratchet. Plus the family-blind view of a request: `PlaneFacts`.
Everything core does *to* a request — govern it, meter it, admit it, screen it, audit it, route it —
reads Layer 1 and nothing else.

**LAYER 2 — family vocabulary.** The IR, opt-in. Messages, roles, slots, turns, tools, `max_tokens`.
The LLM family uses it; MCP uses it; a plane that has no such concepts does not implement it and
loses nothing.

The durable cost this shrinks is the reason to do it: **the IR becomes a frozen FAMILY contract, not
a frozen UNIVERSAL one.** Freezing "a request carries messages and tools and produces a response" as
the universal currency means the contract can only ever express one family. Freezing it as the LLM
family's vocabulary costs exactly what it should.

## 1.2 Why the IR cannot be the universal currency — the closed-enum argument

`IrReq` (`crates/busbar-core/src/ir/variant.rs:39`) is a closed enum of nine variants — `Chat`,
`Embeddings`, `Moderation`, `Image`, `Transcription`, `Speech`, `Rerank`, `Invoke`, `Subscribe`. It
is also `pub(crate)` to `busbar-core`, so it is not merely closed, it is **unreachable from
`busbar-api`** — a plugin could not name it if it wanted to.

A plugin cannot add a variant without editing core. `proto/registry.rs`'s own header already
convicted this pattern, one layer up:

> **"A REGISTRY WHOSE POPULATION IS A `match` IN CORE HAS NOT REMOVED THE MATCH, IT HAS MOVED IT."**

An enum is a match. Protocols-as-plugins is impossible at the IR layer while that enum is the
currency, for precisely the reason it was impossible at the declaration layer before `ProtocolDecl`
made declarations **data**. The registry's fix is the template: whatever core dispatches on becomes
data the plugin supplies.

`IrReq::operation()` (`variant.rs:62`), `wants_stream()` (`:81`) and `prepare_for_egress()` (`:105`)
are each an exhaustive match, and the module header states plainly that this is deliberate — adding
an operation *should* be a compile error at every one. That is the right property for a **family**
contract with a known membership. It is a fatal property for a **universal** one whose membership is
by definition unknown.

## 1.3 The open interface already exists — and it is two-thirds right

`IrFacts` (`crates/busbar-core/src/ir/facts.rs:273`) is what governance, hooks, audit and metering
actually read. Its own header calls it *"the family-blind seam the pipeline reads through."* **Core
needs `IrFacts`, not `IrReq`, and that is already true today.** The enum exists so the egress codec
can dispatch — and a plugin that owns both its reader and its writer makes that its own business.

But `IrFacts` is not family-blind, and checking rather than assuming is what found it. Five methods,
three generalise and two do not — and the two that do not are the two that carry the payload:

| method | generalises? | why |
|---|---|---|
| `verb() -> Operation` | **no** — closed enum | same closed-vocabulary defect, one level down |
| `wants_stream() -> bool` | yes | |
| `end_user() -> Option<&str>` | yes | |
| `shape() -> Shape` | **no** | `{turn_count, has_tools, tool_count, text_chars, system_chars, max_tokens}` is a **chat body**. Conversation turns, tool definitions, a system slot, an output cap in tokens. A packet-oriented plane can answer none of it. |
| `content() -> Vec<ContentItem>` | **no** | `ContentItem { role: IrRole, slot: Slot, .. }` where `IrRole` is `System \| User \| Assistant \| Tool`. Chat roles, in the universal seam. |

The evidence that this was shaped by its two implementors rather than designed: `IrFacts` has exactly
**three** impls in the entire repo — `IrRequest` (`ir/facts.rs:303`), `InvokeReq`
(`ir/invoke.rs:85`), and one test double (`hooks/tests/gate_tests.rs:315`). Both real implementors
are chat-shaped. The seam is family-blind in the way a room is tidy when only its owner has ever
been in it.

## 1.4 `PlaneFacts` — the replacement

Declared this branch at `crates/api/src/plane/facts.rs:90`. It keeps the three that generalise and
replaces the two that do not:

```rust
pub trait PlaneFacts {
    fn verb(&self) -> &str;                        // OPEN. A plane's own word, not a core enum.
    fn wants_stream(&self) -> bool;
    fn end_user(&self) -> Option<&str>;
    fn magnitude(&self) -> Magnitude;              // replaces Shape
    fn screenable(&self) -> Vec<Screenable<'_>>;   // replaces content()
}
```

* **`verb() -> &str`.** The bounded-vocabulary objection is real and is answered by **bounding it by
  DECLARATION rather than by a core enum**: `PlaneDecl` already carries `verbs: &'static [&'static
  str]` (`proto/registry.rs:98` has the same field on `ProtocolDecl`), so the set is finite, known at
  load, and safe as a metric label — without a `match` in core.
* **`Magnitude { unit, amount, caller_cap }`** (`facts.rs:52`). Core needs a number and the name of
  what is counted, for pre-admission budget refusal. It does not need to know whether the number came
  from conversation turns, packet bytes or connection seconds. A2A already meters in **tasks and
  hops** (`a2a/meter.rs`), not tokens — so this is not a hypothetical generalisation, it is the
  existing situation finally being expressible.
* **`Screenable { Text \| Opaque }`** (`facts.rs:71`) carries a `label` — the plane's own word
  (`"message"`, `"tool_arguments"`, `"task_payload"`, `"packet"`) — and deliberately **no role and no
  slot**, because those are chat concepts. `Opaque` preserves `ContentItem::Opaque`'s existing
  contract exactly: the marker stands in and the bytes never leave the plane.

Roles, slots, turns and tools are a **Layer 2 refinement** the LLM family adds on top. They are real
and useful *there* and meaningless elsewhere, and that is the entire content of the layering claim.

# PART 2 — VALIDATING THE SPLIT AGAINST A2A

The kickoff is right that this is the test that matters: *"A2A is that case in production today."*
Verified rather than assumed —

```
grep -rn "IrReq\|IrFacts" crates/busbar-core/src/a2a/    →  0 matches
```

Zero hits across all **35** A2A modules. `IrFacts`'s three impls include no A2A type; `IrReq`'s nine
variants include nothing A2A-shaped. **The claim is factually true.** A2A is a plane, in production,
with no IR — and it was built that way on purpose: `a2a/mod.rs:7-16` states the rule that *"a plane
earns [an intermediate representation] at its SECOND wire format and not before."*

## 2.1 The prediction

If the split is right, A2A should fall out as a **Layer-1-only** plane: it should need every Layer 1
capability and no Layer 2 one. If A2A needs something that is only expressible in Layer 2, the split
is drawn in the wrong place and this document must say so.

## 2.2 The result — ten of eleven, and the eleventh is the one the split predicted would fail

| capability | layer | what in A2A needs it | fits? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PlaneClock` | 1 | pin TTLs, task timers | ✅ |
| `PlaneJournal` | 1 | `a2a/provenance.rs`, per-task chain over `crate::audit` | ✅ |
| `PlaneTasks` | 1 | `a2a/taskstore.rs` | ✅ |
| `PlaneApprovals` | 1 | `crate::trust` + `a2a/pin.rs` | ✅ |
| `PlaneQuarantine` | 1 | demote-on-drift via `reverify::settle` | ✅ |
| `PlaneGovernance` | 1 | `inbound::authorize`, shared governance ledger | ✅ |
| `PlaneMetering` | 1 | `a2a/meter.rs` — **in tasks and hops, not tokens** | ✅ |
| `PlaneCatalogue` | 1 | `registry::inbound_catalogue` | ✅ |
| `PlaneSecrets` | 1 | `a2a/creds.rs` | ✅ (linked; see §3.6) |
| `PlaneMetrics` | 1 | per-plane series | ✅ |
| **`PlaneEgress`** | **2** | `a2a/transport.rs`, and **gRPC** | ❌ |

**Ten of eleven fit Layer 1, and A2A uses all ten today.** The single failure is `PlaneEgress`,
whose `{pool, method, path, headers, body}` is an HTTP request — and A2A's gRPC binding
(`a2a/grpc.rs`, the generated `/lf.a2a.v1.A2AService/*` surface) does not fit that shape.

**This confirms the split rather than falsifying it.** The layering was assigned before this check
was run: `capability.rs`'s working-tree header already marks `PlaneEgress` as *"LAYER 2 — LLM/agentic
family, and this is a DECISION not an oversight."* The split **predicted in advance which capability
a real non-LLM plane would be unable to use**, and then A2A turned out to be unable to use exactly
that one and only that one. A taxonomy that predicts its own exceptions is doing work.

The fix is already named and should be built: the Layer 1 primitive underneath `PlaneEgress` is a
**byte-channel egress**, `open_upstream(pool) -> duplex byte stream`, with core keeping the SSRF
guard, the breaker and the failover. `PlaneEgress` becomes the convenience form built on it for
planes that really are speaking HTTP. gRPC, a VPN tunnel and a raw socket all use the primitive.

## 2.3 Two further confirmations A2A supplied for free

1. **A shape no `IrReq` variant models.** A2A's `INTERRUPT` comes back as itself and the task is
   persisted paused — `a2a/mod.rs:41-43` calls this *"the NORMAL path rather than an edge case."*
   There is no request/response pair here at all. `IrReq`/`IrResp` cannot express it. `PlaneFacts`
   can, because it describes a unit of work rather than a request.
2. **`PlaneMetering` was found by the VPN stress test and A2A needed it too.** The original design
   assumed core would charge from the *response* — true only when there is a response. That is an LLM
   assumption that got baked in without anyone noticing, and R-O's stress test is what surfaced it.
   This is the cleanest evidence available that the VPN test is doing its job as a **test** rather
   than as a roadmap item.

## 2.4 The VPN stress test, run against the final shape

| VPN property | Layer 1 answer |
|---|---|
| no request/response | `PlaneFacts` describes a unit of work; `serve` yields a duplex `PlaneBody` |
| packet-oriented | `Screenable::Opaque { label: "packet", marker }` — bytes never leave the plane |
| no IR | Layer 2 not implemented; nothing is missing |
| duplex forever | duplex byte streams are Layer 1; `wants_stream() == true` means "continuously" |
| metered in bytes | `Magnitude { unit: "bytes", .. }` + `PlaneMetering::charge(grant, "bytes", n)` |

A VPN plane uses Layer 1 alone. **No Layer 2 concept appears anywhere in that column**, which is the
property R-O asked for.

# PART 3 — THE BIDIRECTIONAL CAPABILITY VTABLE

This is the riskiest unit in the design and it is the one that does not exist yet.

## 3.1 The blocker, stated exactly

`plugin-abi` is **one-directional**: six host→plugin symbols (`busbar_abi`, `busbar_plugin_kind`,
`busbar_open`, `busbar_call`, `busbar_free`, `busbar_close`; `lib.rs:145-164`) and exactly **one**
inbound path — `LogSinkFn` (`lib.rs:193`), installed by the optional seventh symbol
`busbar_set_log_sink` (`lib.rs:161`).

**Capabilities are inbound by definition.** `PlaneGovernance::admit`, `PlaneMetering::charge`,
`PlaneJournal::append` are all the plugin calling the host. Every one of the eleven needs a host
callback that does not exist. This is plugin-invoked code on the trust-critical load path, so the
design has to be argued rather than sketched.

## 3.2 The precedent to extend, and why it is the right one

`LogSinkFn` already solved this problem once, for one function, and its design notes contain three
decisions worth taking wholesale (`lib.rs:187-215`):

1. It is `extern "C"`, **not** `"C-unwind"` — *"this is the host's code called FROM the plugin, and a
   panic unwinding out of it into a differently-compiled object is undefined behaviour. The host
   catches its own panics inside."*
2. It is installed **once**, after a successful `busbar_open` and before any `busbar_call`.
3. Adding it was **not a transport bump**. `TRANSPORT_VERSION` covers the six required signatures and
   the ptr+len rule; a seventh *optional* symbol changes none of them, so already-signed artifacts
   keep loading.

Point 3 is what makes this affordable: **the capability vtable arrives as an eighth optional symbol
and `TRANSPORT_VERSION` stays 1.** No relink event, no side-by-side migration.

## 3.3 The shape

```rust
pub const PLANE_HOST_ABI: u32 = 1;

/// Installed once, after `busbar_open`, before any `busbar_call`.
/// Optional symbol `busbar_set_plane_host\0`.
pub type SetPlaneHostFn =
    unsafe extern "C-unwind" fn(vt: *const PlaneHostVtable, token: u64) -> i32;

#[repr(C)]
pub struct PlaneHostVtable {
    /// `size_of::<PlaneHostVtable>()` AS THE HOST COMPILED IT. Growth is
    /// append-only; a plugin needing a field past `size` refuses at install.
    pub size: usize,
    pub abi: u32,
    pub _reserved: u32,

    // NULL means NOT GRANTED. Grant is per-plane, decided by core at load.
    pub govern_admit:      Option<HostCall>,
    pub meter_charge:      Option<HostCall>,
    pub journal_append:    Option<HostCall>,
    pub journal_read:      Option<HostCall>,
    pub tasks_op:          Option<HostCall>,
    pub approvals_redeem:  Option<HostCall>,
    pub quarantine_op:     Option<HostCall>,
    pub catalogue_op:      Option<HostCall>,
    pub egress_open:       Option<HostCall>,
    pub metrics_emit:      Option<HostCall>,
    pub clock_now:         Option<HostCall>,
    // NOTE: there is deliberately NO `secrets_resolve`. See §3.6.

    pub free: unsafe extern "C" fn(token: u64, ptr: *mut u8, len: usize),
}

/// EVERY inbound call has this one signature. Nothing else crosses inbound.
pub type HostCall = unsafe extern "C" fn(
    token: u64,             // NOT a pointer. See §3.5.
    req: *const u8, req_len: usize,
    out: *mut *mut u8, out_len: *mut usize,
) -> i32;
```

Five properties are doing the work, and each is a deliberate refusal of an easier option.

## 3.4 Property 1 — no unwinding, ever, inbound

Every `HostCall` is `extern "C"`, matching `LogSinkFn` verbatim and for its stated reason. The host
side of each callback wraps its body in `catch_unwind` and converts a panic into `STATUS_PANIC`
(`lib.rs:264`, which already exists). **A panic never crosses inbound**; it becomes an integer.

The asymmetry with the six host→plugin symbols (which are `"C-unwind"`) is deliberate and already
precedented: the host can afford to let a plugin's unwind pass through frames the host compiled,
because it catches it at a frame it owns. The reverse is not true.

Two consequences that must be handled rather than noted:

* **Lock poisoning.** A host callback that panics while holding a `std::sync::Mutex` poisons it, and
  the next call sees `PoisonError`. Because the plugin can *cause* the panic by feeding malformed
  bytes, poisoning is a plugin-triggerable denial of service on core state. **Rule: inbound callback
  bodies use non-poisoning locks, or handle `PoisonError` explicitly by recovering the guard.**
* **`catch_unwind` is not a sandbox.** It catches unwinding panics; it does not catch `panic =
  "abort"`, stack exhaustion, or a plugin that calls `abort()`. A dlopen'd plugin can always kill the
  process. **That is not a regression and it must be stated: the dlopen boundary is a compatibility
  boundary, not a security boundary.** Everything below assumes a plugin that is malicious about
  *semantics* — forging trust state, under-reporting, escaping scope — because those are the attacks
  the boundary can actually prevent.

## 3.5 Property 2 — the host handle is a TOKEN, not a pointer

`LogSinkFn` takes `ctx: *mut c_void` and requires that *"the sink must outlive the plugin."* For a
log sink that rule is affordable. For eleven capabilities that mutate trust state it is not, because
the failure mode is a use-after-free **on the trust-critical path** — a plugin background thread
holding `ctx` after core dropped the plane instance.

So: **`ctx` becomes `token: u64`.** A generational index into a host-side slab. Every callback begins
by resolving token → slot and checking the generation, and returns `STATUS_GONE` on a stale one.

The consequence is the strongest soundness property in the design:

> **NO HOST CALLBACK EVER DEREFERENCES A PLUGIN-SUPPLIED POINTER AS A RUST OBJECT.** Every inbound
> argument is a scalar or a byte range. A use-after-free becomes a returned error code, and a forged
> token becomes a generation mismatch.

A plugin can therefore lie about a token and get `STATUS_GONE`; it cannot lie about a token and get
another plane's `PlaneCtx`. It can hand a garbage `req` pointer and cause a segfault **in its own
address space semantics** — the host validates `req_len` against `MAX_PLUGIN_RESPONSE_LEN`
(`lib.rs:227`) before reading and copies before parsing, so a truncated or oversized buffer is a
refusal, not a read past the end.

## 3.6 Property 3 — scope is BOUND, never passed; and `PlaneSecrets` is not in the vtable

Scope is already bound rather than passed for the linked form: *"None of them takes a scope parameter
the plane could lie about: the scope is bound when core constructs the handle"* (`capability.rs`).
The token preserves this exactly — the slot the token resolves to **is** the scope. There is no
plane key on the wire for a plugin to forge.

**`secrets_resolve` is deliberately absent from the vtable.** This is the sharpest constraint in the
kickoff and it deserves to be honoured rather than weakened:

> `PlaneSecrets` cannot be narrowed — `Redacted<String>` is a Rust guarantee that survives no
> boundary. That is ratification policy, not a signature.

Across a C ABI, `Redacted<String>` is bytes. The type does nothing. So putting `secrets_resolve` in
the vtable would materialise plaintext inside a dlopen'd plugin with no enforcement of any kind.

**This appears to collide with R-K** — *compiled-in vs dlopen is a packaging choice, not a capability
limit* — and the resolution matters, so here it is explicitly. `Redacted` is not a capability; it is
an *enforcement mechanism* for one. The capability a plane actually needs is **authenticated egress**,
not **possession of a secret**. And busbar already has the right shape for that, in `api/auth.rs`'s
`LoginHop`: *"an HTTP hop the module DESCRIBES and the CORE EXECUTES"* — **the module writes the form
KEY, never the secret.**

So: a dlopen'd plane passes a **secret reference** into `egress_open`, and **core resolves it at the
socket**. The plane never sees plaintext, in either packaging. R-K is satisfied — the capability is
available in both forms — and the constraint is satisfied, because the plaintext never crosses.
Linked planes may keep `PlaneSecrets::resolve`, where `Redacted` is a real guarantee.

## 3.7 Property 4 — re-entrancy is bounded, and core holds no lock across `busbar_call`

The call graph is now cyclic: core → `busbar_call` → plugin → `HostCall` → core. Two hazards:

* **Deadlock.** If core holds a lock across `busbar_call` and the same lock is taken by a host
  callback, the plugin deadlocks core by doing the ordinary thing. **Invariant: core holds no lock
  across a `busbar_call`.** This is mechanically checkable and should get a `structure-lint.sh` row,
  the same way `A8-protocol-decl-for` already scans `decl_for` for allocations.
* **Unbounded recursion.** A malicious plugin can call `govern_admit` from inside a callback
  reachable from `govern_admit`. Host callbacks are **leaf operations by contract** — they never call
  back into the plugin — so the cycle can only be plugin-driven, and a per-token re-entrancy depth
  counter refuses beyond depth 1. Cheap, and it turns a stack-exhaustion abort into `STATUS_ERR`.

## 3.8 Property 5 — grants are core-minted and unforgeable

`PlaneGovernance::admit` returns `PlaneGrant { token }`, and `PlaneEgress` requires one. If a plugin
could mint a grant it would have ungoverned egress. So a grant is a core-minted opaque value, MAC'd
or slab-indexed, single-use, and validated by core at the point of use. **The plugin can hold a
grant; it cannot construct one.**

## 3.9 What a malicious plugin can do with each callback — the honest table

| callback | worst case | why that is acceptable, or what bounds it |
|---|---|---|
| `clock_now` | reads the clock repeatedly | nothing |
| `metrics_emit` | series spam | core prefixes every series with the plane key, so it can neither collide with a first-party series nor forge another plane's |
| `journal_append` | writes garbage; floods | **cannot forge the chain.** Digests are computed engine-side; the plugin supplies subject + payload bytes and core supplies `prev_hash`/`hash`. This is the constraint that makes a raw `Store` grant unacceptable, preserved exactly. Flooding is rate-limited per token. |
| `journal_read` | reads its own journal | scope-bound; cannot read another plane's |
| `tasks_op` | corrupts its own task store | it already owns those tasks |
| `approvals_redeem` | burns a nonce | scope-bound; burning its own nonces is self-harm |
| `quarantine_op` | demotes everything in its own plane | it already controls that plane; scope prevents demoting a rival's upstream |
| `catalogue_op` | reads its own catalogue | scope-bound |
| `govern_admit` | asks for admission in a loop → ledger spam / DoS | rate-limited per token; **and admission is not a licence to work** — the grant is opaque, single-use, and validated at the point of use |
| `egress_open` | SSRF | **the plane names a POOL, never a URL.** Core keeps resolve-then-pin (`crate::net_guard`), the breaker, and failover. Unchanged from the linked design. |
| `meter_charge` | **under-reports consumption** | **see below — this one is not fully mitigable and must be ruled on.** |

### The one that does not close: self-reported metering

A plane is the only party that knows how much work it did, so `meter_charge` is **self-reported by
construction**. A malicious or merely buggy plane can charge zero. Core can bound it (a plane cannot
charge *another* plane, because scope is bound) but cannot verify it.

**The finding, stated so it is not discovered later by an auditor: a self-reporting meter is not an
audit source.** Anything requiring non-repudiation must be metered by core at a chokepoint core owns
— bytes through core's egress, requests through core's door — not by the plane's own `charge` call.
`PlaneMetering` is the right capability for *budgets and operator visibility*; it is the wrong
capability for *billing a customer who may dispute it*. A2A already got this right by accident:
`a2a/meter.rs:1-5` records that ingress meters *"through the shared governance ledger rather than a
second one."*

**This needs the owner's ruling** (§6, item 1): is `PlaneMetering` advisory-only, or does billing-
grade metering require a core-owned chokepoint for every plane?

## 3.10 THE BORROW CONTRACT — condition (2) of the Crossing Theorem

This subsection exists because §0.2 proved it has to. Without it the archive format buys a 24x slope
improvement and **not** the latency bar.

### 3.10.1 Why the copy is there in the first place

The frozen contract is *plugin allocates → host copies out → host calls `busbar_free`*. **The copy is
not laziness; it is allocator isolation.** A dlopen'd plugin may be linked against a different
allocator than the host, so the host must never `free` a plugin pointer and vice versa. Copying out
and handing the original back to `busbar_free` is the straightforward way to guarantee that, and for
the cold-path kinds — where the ABI is frozen and the doc's own justification is correct — it should
stay exactly as it is.

**Borrow-not-copy does not weaken allocator isolation. It strengthens it**: the plugin's allocator
both allocates *and* frees, and the host's allocator is never involved at all.

### 3.10.2 The shape

```rust
/// The plugin writes a descriptor of a buffer IT STILL OWNS. The host does not copy.
#[repr(C)]
pub struct PlaneBorrow {
    pub ptr: *const u8,
    pub len: usize,
    /// Plugin-chosen, handed back verbatim to `release`. Lets the plugin use an
    /// arena/slab index instead of a raw allocation.
    pub cookie: u64,
    /// The alignment the plugin GUARANTEES for `ptr`. The host verifies the pointer
    /// rather than trusting this number, and refuses a borrow too weakly aligned for
    /// the archive format. Alignment must be in the contract — `ptr + len` says
    /// nothing about it, and a one-byte opcode prefix would misalign every archive
    /// and force back exactly the copy this exists to remove.
    pub align: u32,
    pub _pad: u32,
}

pub type PlaneCallFn = unsafe extern "C-unwind" fn(
    handle: *mut c_void, req: *const u8, req_len: usize, out: *mut PlaneBorrow,
) -> i32;

/// Called by the host EXACTLY ONCE per successful call, from a Drop guard.
pub type PlaneReleaseFn =
    unsafe extern "C-unwind" fn(handle: *mut c_void, borrow: *const PlaneBorrow);
```

### 3.10.3 The soundness argument

**Who owns the buffer?** The plugin, for its entire lifetime. The host never takes ownership, never
frees, never reallocs, never writes. Ownership never transfers, so there is no double-free and no
cross-allocator free.

**When is release called?** Exactly once, from a host-side `PlaneBorrowGuard` whose `Drop` calls
`release`. `Drop` runs on the panic path too, so a host panic mid-read still releases. The guard
hands out `&'a [u8]` with a lifetime tied to itself, so **no host code can stash the slice past the
guard** — that part is enforced by the Rust compiler rather than by review.

**What if the host never releases?** The plugin leaks. That is a host bug, and the guard's existence
is the fix; it cannot be forgotten at a call site because the slice cannot be obtained without it.

**What if the plugin frees early, or never?** *Never* is the plugin leaking its own memory — self-harm,
bounded by its own footprint, and attributable to it in RSS. *Early* means the host reads freed
memory, which is undefined behaviour and **the boundary cannot prevent it.** This belongs in the same
paragraph as `abort()` (§3.4): a plugin that frees a live borrow is indistinguishable from a plugin
that corrupts memory directly, and the dlopen boundary is a compatibility boundary, not a security
boundary. What narrows the window is that between `busbar_call` returning and `release` being called,
**the plugin has no thread of control the host invoked** — so only a plugin *background* thread can do
it, which is the honest statement of the residual risk.

**What can a malicious plugin do with a borrow?** It cannot *hold* one open — the host owns the
release. So the attacks are all lies in the descriptor, and each is closed by an **O(1)** check,
which matters because an O(n) check would reintroduce the linearity the borrow exists to remove:

| lie | consequence | check |
|---|---|---|
| `len` exceeds the real buffer | host reads out of bounds | `len <= MAX_PLUGIN_RESPONSE_LEN`, **and** the range lies wholly inside an arena the plugin registered at `open`. O(1). |
| `ptr` outside anything it owns | host segfault | arena containment, same check |
| `align` declared but not honoured | misaligned archive access, UB on some targets | host verifies `ptr % align == 0` itself and never trusts the declared value. O(1). |
| same buffer returned to two calls | aliasing | harmless: the host holds `&[u8]`, shared and immutable |
| **mutates the buffer during the borrow** | **data race on a live `&[u8]`** | **not closable.** The contract declares the buffer immutable for the borrow's lifetime; violation is UB, same bucket as freeing early. |

**Arena registration is what turns "any pointer" into "a pointer in a region I know about,"** and it
is the single piece of hardening worth the complexity. It is O(1) and it converts the most likely
*accidental* failure — a plugin bug producing a stale or wild descriptor — from silent corruption
into a refusal.

**Re-entrancy.** At most one outstanding borrow per handle, enforced host-side by the guard. The
plugin may rely on this and use a single scratch arena. The §3.7 rule that core holds no lock across
`busbar_call` extends to holding no borrow across a second one.

### 3.10.4 Why this actually restores flatness

Borrowing a 405 KB buffer and reading 200 bytes of it touches **~2 pages**. Copying it touches
**~100**. That is the whole difference, and it is why condition (2) is about the *transfer* rather
than about the format: the format decides what you must parse, the transport decides what you must
touch.

# PART 4 — THE WIRE FORMAT

## 4.1 What is actually being chosen

Not "JSON vs FlatBuffers". Three separate questions that the kickoff's framing merges:

1. **What does core read?** — Layer 1 facts. Bounded, small, fixed by the contract.
2. **What does core forward without reading?** — the body. Unbounded.
3. **What does the plugin do with its own IR?** — its own business. It owns both reader and writer.
4. **How do the bytes get from the plugin to core?** — the *transport*, which is a separate axis from
   the *format* and the one §0.2 shows dominates once the format is fixed. Copy-then-free is O(bytes)
   whatever the format; borrow-then-release is O(1). §3.10.

Once (1) and (2) are separated, the format question has an easy answer and the hard part was the
separation.

## 4.2 The choice

* **Layer 1 facts: FlatBuffers, verified.** Flat in payload bytes (§0), sound, keeps both stated
  reasons for JSON — additive version tolerance via explicit field IDs, and language-agnosticism via
  a schema any language can compile.
* **The body: an untraversed `[ubyte]` vector in the same buffer.** One crossing, one allocation. The
  verifier range-checks it and does not walk it. Core points at it and hands it on.
* **Layer 2 IR: never crosses as a verified whole.** It rides inside the body. The plugin builds the
  archived buffer as its **native output** — it parses the incoming wire once, directly into the
  buffer, rather than into structs it then encodes.

**Rejected, with reasons:**

| option | why not |
|---|---|
| JSON on the hot path | linear in payload bytes because it is a parse; ~180 µs at 65 KB, **~1.0 ms at 405 KB** |
| FlatBuffers, whole IR, verified | **linear** — ~28 µs at 65 KB and **~198 µs at 405 KB**. Zero-copy alone does not meet the bar. |
| FlatBuffers, whole IR, **unverified** | flat and fast but **unsound** (~6-14 ns at every size): `root_as_*_unchecked` on a buffer authored by an untrusted plugin hands out typed references derived from attacker-controlled offsets. This is the trap, and it is an attractive one because the number is beautiful. |
| rkyv | validated access is also linear (~3.8 µs at 65 KB, ~17 µs at 405 KB — better than FlatBuffers but the same shape), and it is **weaker on version tolerance**, which is one of the two reasons JSON was chosen in the first place. Faster is not the axis that was in doubt. **And the decisive objection is safety, not speed: a wrong hand-written bytecheck bound silently downgrades a validated read to an unvalidated one** (§4.4 finding 5) — the design's central trap, reachable by a typo in a type-level bound rather than by an explicit `_unchecked` call. |
| Cap'n Proto | same structural answer as FlatBuffers; not measured. Choosing between them is a tooling decision, not a design one, and either satisfies condition (1) of the Crossing Theorem. Neither satisfies condition (2) — no format can, because the copy is in the transport. |

**The store/secret/auth/hook/export ABI keeps JSON and is not touched.** Its justification is correct
for its purpose and R-N freezes it.

## 4.3 The measurements

**PoC:** `docs/design/poc/plane-crossing/` — a standalone crate, deliberately outside the workspace.
It uses **flatc-generated code** (flatc 25.12.19, flatbuffers 25), **real rkyv 0.8**, and a **real
`dlopen`'d cdylib** for the bare linkage floor. It exists because the in-tree
`marshal_cost_tests.rs` hand-rolls its zero-copy side with a bare offset table, which flatters the
result in exactly two ways this PoC removes: it never builds the buffer inside the measurement, and
**it never verifies**. An unverified read is not a crossing you can put a trust boundary on, so
measuring one answers a question nobody asked.

**Profile: `--release`, `lto = true`, `codegen-units = 1`.** Debug is ~16x and exists in no shipped
build. flatc 25.12.19 / flatbuffers 25 / rkyv 0.8. Apple Silicon, macOS 25.5.

**Contention, stated because a number without it is half a number.** The fleet run for 1.6.0 had
7-8 concurrent agents doing `cargo` gate builds throughout. Load average during this run: **24.7 at
start, 18.6 at end**. Every cell is `median [min–max]` over repeats, and **the spread is the honest
column** — under this load the absolute values are inflated and the min is the least contaminated
estimate. What is *not* contaminated is the **shape** of each column, because contention scales a
whole run together; and the shape is the entire argument.

**ns per VERIFY+READ** — *not* per crossing. The buffer is built outside the timing loop, so no
allocation, `memcpy`, free or DSO transit is inside the measured region. Add the transport arm from
§0.2 (~0.03 ns/B) to get an end-to-end crossing under the *current* contract; under the §3.10 borrow
contract the transport term is what goes away.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 629 B | 2,992 | 6,616 | 1,645 | 13.1 | 722 | 27 | **111** |

Stream frame (84 B): JSON crossing **386 ns**; offset read across a real DSO **1.3 ns**.

**Read the last column.** Across a **645x** range in payload size — 629 B to 405 KB, deliberately
past L2 so the result cannot be dismissed as cache residency — the sound, fully-verified Layer 1
crossing stays between **75 and 112 ns**, and it is *lower* at 405 KB than at 629 B. Every other
column tracks the payload. At 405 KB the Layer 1 crossing is **~13,700x** cheaper than the JSON
crossing busbar uses today and **~2,600x** cheaper than verifying the whole IR zero-copy.

That is the bar met — nanoseconds, not microseconds — on a **contended** machine, which means the
margin is not an artifact of a favourable measurement environment.

**What would falsify this:** an L1 facts column that tracked payload size. It does not, at any size
tested, under load or otherwise. **What it does not establish, and an earlier draft wrongly claimed
it did:** that the end-to-end crossing is flat. See §0.2.

## 4.4 FIVE FINDINGS FROM THE MEASUREMENT BRANCH, all adopted

`perf/1.6.0-plugin-abi-measurement` (`73de73c7`) fitted slopes over 10 points across 2.5 decades,
which is a better instrument than this document's size sweep. Its findings are adopted, not merely
noted, and two of them change design decisions.

**1. Stop optimising the call.** At a 10 KB crossing: cross-DSO call **8.8 ns (0.04%)**, payload
transport **269 ns (1.1%)**, serialization **23,528 ns (98.8%)**. Its 8.8 ns and this document's
0.9 ns (§4.5) differ only in what marshalling they include and **agree in kind**: the linkage was
never the cost. No further work on call overhead is justified by any number either of us has.

**2. "Proportional to payload bytes" is an incomplete model — and this strengthens the layer split.**
Holding byte count to ±9% while moving JSON *value* count 27x, a two-term fit holds to 1.9% worst
residual:

> **t = 0.93 ns/byte + 21.9 ns per JSON value** — one value ≈ 23 bytes equivalent, stable at 22–23
> across a 3x load range.

So **a structurally dense 32 KB request costs 1.8x a sparse one of identical size.** The expensive
shape is tool schemas and long message histories — *precisely the material Layer 1 does not read*.
The facts are structurally sparse by construction: a handful of scalars and short strings. The kickoff's
"cost is proportional to payload bytes" understated the case for the split rather than overstating it.

**3. The cheapest large win needs no zero-copy at all — adopt it as the first step.** `rawbytes→rkyv`,
i.e. hand the plugin the caller's **unparsed** HTTP body: at 10 KB it costs 4,633 ns against the
dialect's own in-process parse of 4,410 ns — a **marginal boundary cost of ~220 ns for work the
dialect had to do anyway.** This is the honest answer for unary traffic and it requires no producer
to emit archives natively. It becomes step 6 of §5, ahead of the archive work.

**4. Alignment must be in the contract.** `ptr + len` says nothing about alignment; rkyv needs 16 B;
a one-byte opcode prefix misaligns every archive and forces back exactly the copy zero-copy exists to
remove. Adopted into `PlaneBorrow::align` (§3.10.2), with the host verifying the pointer rather than
trusting the declared value.

**5. Two correctness traps in the IR — and one of them changes the format choice.**

* `IrBlock::ToolResult::content` is a `Vec<IrBlock>`, so the rkyv derive overflows the trait solver
  (20 × E0275) and needs `#[rkyv(omit_bounds)]` with hand-written bounds. **Getting the bytecheck
  bound wrong silently downgrades a validated read to an unvalidated one.** That is a soundness hole
  that looks like it works, on the trust-critical path, detectable only by reading the bounds — and
  §4.2 already rejects unvalidated reads as the design's central trap. **A format whose safety
  property can be silently lost in a type-level bound is a worse fit for this boundary than one whose
  verifier is generated code that cannot be accidentally disabled.** This is a substantive point for
  FlatBuffers over rkyv, independent of version tolerance, and it is added to §4.2's table.
* **Six IR sites are `serde_json::Value`**, which no archived format has. They become opaque JSON
  *text*, so a plugin that genuinely reads a tool schema pays back the parse the archive removed.
  This is consistent with the design — the body is opaque and core never reads it — but it means
  **Layer 2 planes do not get the archive's benefit on those fields** and should not be promised it.
* Validation itself is affordable: checked 3,453 ns vs unchecked 2,286 ns at 10 KB (~1.5x, still ~7x
  better than JSON). **It must not be shipped unchecked for a dropped-in plugin** — which is §4.2's
  position, now with a price tag attached showing the position is cheap to hold.


## 4.5 The claim about the bare crossing, confirmed

*"A linked library is not slower than native. A cross-DSO call is a PLT indirection — single-digit
nanoseconds."* Measured across a real `dlopen`'d boundary: **sub-nanosecond to ~1 ns**, and an
offset read performed *inside* the plugin across that boundary is ~1.5 ns.

**So the linkage was never the cost. Everything in every table above is what busbar chose to put on
the boundary.** That was the kickoff's framing and it survives contact with the measurement intact.

# PART 5 — MIGRATION

Ordered so that each step is independently valuable and none of them is a flag day. Note that steps
0-3 involve **no C ABI change at all** — R-K says packaging is a choice, so the linked form ships
first and the dlopen form follows the proof.

| # | step | ABI risk | what it proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Land `PlaneFacts` + `PlaneMetering` in `busbar-api`. Add `metering` to `PlaneCtx` (**missing today** — the trait is declared and re-exported but not plumbed). | none | the Layer 1 vocabulary compiles and is implementable |
| 1 | **A2A implements `PlaneFacts`** over its existing types. Nothing else changes. | none | the split is real, against a plane with no IR, in production |
| 2 | The **MCP split**: `askstate` / `calllog` / `demotion` become core-side `PlaneApprovals` / `PlaneCallLog` / `PlaneQuarantine`. | none | the engine's trust state is the engine's, not MCP's — and A2A needs the same three |
| 3 | Core reads `PlaneFacts` instead of `IrFacts` at the governance / hook / audit / metering seams. `IrReq` survives as the **LLM family's** enum and stops being the universal currency. | none | **condition (1)** of the Crossing Theorem holds in core |
| 4 | Linked plane plugins via `PlaneDecl` + `PlaneHandler`. Capabilities are Rust trait objects. | none | R-E/R-F/R-H: a plane depends on `busbar-api` alone |
| 5 | The **byte-channel egress primitive**; `PlaneEgress` rebuilt on it. | none | A2A's gRPC and a VPN plane both become expressible |
| 6 | The **host vtable** — optional symbol `busbar_set_plane_host`. `TRANSPORT_VERSION` **stays 1** (precedent: `busbar_set_log_sink`). | additive only | dlopen'd planes; R-K closed |
| 7 | **`rawbytes` ingress**: hand the plane the caller's UNPARSED body. No archive, no producer changes. | additive | **~220 ns marginal** over work the dialect did anyway (§4.4 finding 3) — the cheapest large win in the plan, and it lands before any format work |
| 8 | The **borrow transport** — `PlaneBorrow` + `busbar_plane_release`, replacing copy-then-`busbar_free` **for the plane kind only**. Frozen kinds keep copy semantics untouched. | **new optional call shape**, plane kind only | **condition (2)** of the Crossing Theorem. Without this the archive buys a 24x slope and not the bar. |
| 9 | FlatBuffers Layer 1 facts on the dlopen wire, with `align` honoured. | additive; negotiated by manifest `abi_version` | the latency bar, end to end |

**Step 7 before steps 8-9 is deliberate.** `rawbytes` delivers most of the practical win for unary
traffic at ~220 ns marginal cost and requires nothing of producers. If it proves sufficient in
production, steps 8-9 can be deferred on evidence rather than abandoned on a guess — and if it does
not, the measurement that says so will be a production one.

**The reversibility argument.** Steps 0-7 are ordinary Rust refactors inside crates busbar owns, and
each is revertible. The one no-turning-back event in the whole plan — a `TRANSPORT_VERSION` bump — is
**avoided entirely** by the optional-symbol precedent. There is no point in this sequence where
already-signed store, auth, secret, hook or export plugins stop loading.

# PART 6 — WHAT NEEDS THE OWNER'S RULING

1. **Is `PlaneMetering` advisory, or must billing-grade metering run through a core-owned
   chokepoint?** (§3.9.) A self-reported meter cannot be an audit source. This is the only place in
   the design where a stated constraint cannot be fully mechanised, and it is better decided now than
   discovered by a customer's auditor.
2. **Does `secrets_resolve`-by-reference satisfy R-K?** (§3.6.) I have read R-K as *the capability
   must be available in both packagings*, not *the same signature must be*, and resolved the
   `Redacted` constraint by moving secret resolution to the socket. If R-K means the literal
   signature, then dlopen'd planes cannot have secrets at all and that should be said out loud
   instead.
3. **Should `IrReq` be renamed when it stops being universal?** It becomes the LLM family's enum in
   step 3. Leaving it called `IrReq` invites the next reader to make exactly the mistake this
   document is undoing. `LlmReq` costs one commit now and cannot be bought later.
4. **Is the borrow transport (§3.10) approved in principle?** It is a *transport-shape* change, not
   merely a format change, and it is the difference between meeting the latency bar and getting a 24x
   slope improvement that is still linear. R-N permits it for the plane kind and the frozen kinds are
   untouched — but it introduces one hazard the boundary cannot close (a plugin freeing or mutating a
   live borrow, §3.10.3), in the same class as `abort()`. **If that residual is unacceptable, the
   honest consequence is that the bar cannot be met for dlopen'd planes and can only be met for
   linked ones**, and the design should say so rather than quietly ship the copy.
5. **FlatBuffers or Cap'n Proto.** Structurally equivalent for this purpose (§4.2). FlatBuffers is
   measured here and its Rust toolchain is in better shape; I have no strong view and it is a
   reversible tooling call, but it should be made deliberately rather than by default.

# PART 7 — WHAT IS NOT PROVEN, so its absence is not read as a claim

* **No dlopen'd plane was built or loaded.** The host vtable is designed and argued in §3; it is not
  implemented. Its soundness argument is a design argument, not a test result.
* **The PoC measures the READ HALF only, not a whole crossing** (§0.2). It shows that a bounded
  verified read is flat and an unbounded one is linear. It does **not** measure allocation, `memcpy`,
  free or DSO transit, and an earlier draft of this document wrongly presented its figures as
  end-to-end crossing costs. The transport term comes from `perf/1.6.0-plugin-abi-measurement`.
* **Nobody has measured the borrow transport.** Condition (2) of the Crossing Theorem is argued from
  the transport arm's slope (a copy is O(bytes); not copying removes that term) and from the page-touch
  argument in §3.10.4. **It has not been built or timed.** That measurement is the first thing step 8
  should produce, and if it does not show the transport term vanishing, §0.2 is wrong in the other
  direction and the doc must be revised again.
* **The PoC does not show busbar's actual governance seam reads only bounded facts** — that is step
  3's job, and until it lands, condition (1) is a design intention rather than a property of the tree.
* **`PlaneFacts` is declared and unit-tested, and wired to nothing.** It is not referenced by
  `PlaneHandler`, `PlaneCtx` or `PlaneDecl`. It is also **not `Send + Sync`**, unlike every
  capability trait; that is probably wrong and should be settled when it is first wired.
* **`PlaneCtx` has no `metering` field.** `PlaneMetering` is declared and re-exported only.
* **Cap'n Proto was not measured.**
* **A2A validated the split on paper**, by mapping its existing modules onto the capability list. It
  does not yet implement `PlaneFacts`, and until step 1 lands, §2 is a strong argument rather than a
  demonstration.
* **The `catch_unwind` boundary is not a sandbox** (§3.4). A dlopen'd plugin can abort the process.
  The dlopen boundary is a compatibility boundary, not a security boundary, and no claim here should
  be read as saying otherwise.
