## 1. What already exists, and what supersedes what

`crates/busbar-contract/src/count.rs` — `Count`, an `i128` mantissa at one fixed scale of 6.

* Construct: `Count::parse(&str)` / `Count::parse_bytes(&[u8])` from decimal TEXT,
  `Count::from_integer(i128)`, `Count::from_micros(i128)`, `Count::from_stored(value, scale)`.
* Read from a body: `Count::read_at(body: &[u8], pointer: &str)` — resolves through the closed span
  grammar (`busbar_contract::spans`) and parses the value's own bytes.
* The usage seam: `read_count(&str)` / `read_count_bytes(&[u8])` — `parse` plus "a measurement is
  not below zero".
* Arithmetic: `checked_add`, `checked_sub`, `checked_mul` (exact-or-`Inexact`), `checked_mul_wide`
  (the exact scale-12 product, for summing before any rounding decision), `checked_mul_integer`,
  `Count::total`. All checked; none wraps and none saturates.
* Render: `to_decimal_string()` / `Display` — one canonical spelling per quantity.
* Persist: `stored_value()` (the mantissa) beside `SCALE_MICRO_UNITS`; read back through
  `from_stored`. `stored_scale_default()` is what a record's `#[serde(default = "…")]` names.
* Whole-unit destinations: `whole_units()` returns `Err(CountError::NotWhole)` for a fraction —
  #81a(f)'s refusal, never a truncation.

**Two seams are superseded, not one.** Both are byte-for-byte the same function under different
names, and both are still live — **do not delete either until its call sites move**:

| superseded | call sites (production) |
|---|---|
| `crates/busbar-llm-codec/src/usage_count.rs::read_count_u64` | 109 |
| `crates/busbar-voice-codec/src/ir/usage.rs::read_count_u64` | 9 (2 of them the definition and its own doc reference) |

(118 production references total, measured; `usage.rs`'s own header already states it "carries the
identical seam".) A converter that fixes only the first has fixed one of two.

**MEASURED DIVERGENCE BETWEEN THE TWO COPIES — do not pick a survivor without porting these.**

* **The function bodies are the same code.** Diffed at HEAD: the only difference is where a comment
  wraps. No behavioural divergence in `read_count_u64` itself.
* **The histories are not.** The LLM copy has taken three money fixes the audio copy never saw:
  `834dc1836` (the seam itself), `812370e09` (two more dialects routed through it), and `3c321a31e`
  (the in-crate guard that stops the next dialect reinventing the zeroing read). The audio copy was
  created by a crate split (`522694e82`) and has received none of them.
* **The audio copy carries NO guard test at all** — measured, zero. That is blindness 2 stated as a
  fact about the tree rather than a worry: the crate the guard existed to protect against was the
  one crate the guard could not see.
* **Surface now differs**: the LLM copy carries `billed_count` + `UnreadableCount` (absent is zero,
  unreadable is a refusal); the audio copy carries only the old `Option<u64>` seam and still ends
  five billed reads in `.unwrap_or(0)`. **This asymmetry was introduced deliberately and is
  temporary** — the audio crate is inside a live crate fold and must not be edited across it.

So the consolidation is a PORT, not a merge choice: one implementation survives and it must be the
one carrying the guard, the refusal and the three fixes. Neither copy differs from the other on what
a count MEANS, so there is no defect to adjudicate — only debt to collapse.

## 2. THE SILENT ZERO — HALF FIXED, HALF STILL LIVE

> **STATUS UPDATE.** The eight billed reads in `busbar-llm-codec`'s own handlers were fixed in
> `3c533603f`: `usage_count::billed_count` keeps absence (zero, unchanged) apart from unreadability
> (a refusal, `CodecError::Malformed`). The FIVE in `busbar-voice-codec` are still live and still
> bill zero; that crate is inside a live crate fold and must not be edited across it. The rest of
> this section is the reasoning, and it still governs the remaining five and every future site.

### The original finding: `None` currently means ZERO, not "skip"

`read_count_u64` returns `Option<u64>`, and the call sites end in `.unwrap_or(0)` — **40 of them, measured, across
eleven files** (44 if any default, not just a zero, is counted). So on trunk today a count the reader refuses is recorded as **zero**,
which is the exact failure #81 exists to end: a refusal is a fact, a zero is a claim that no work
happened.

**The conversion therefore changes the call-site CONTRACT, not just the reader.** `Count`'s reader
returns `Result<_, CountError>`, and the `Err` has to be *handled* — propagated into the unit's
refusal path (#42), never `unwrap_or`'d back into a zero. A mechanical `read_count_u64(…)` →
`read_count(…)` substitution that keeps `.unwrap_or(0)` reproduces the defect with a new spelling and
will read as done.

This is the single highest-risk step in the whole conversion. Budget for it accordingly: it is a
behavioural change at 40 sites, and each one needs a decision about what the unit does when the
provider's count cannot be read.

## 3. HARD SEQUENCING CONSTRAINT: counts, caps and rates move in ONE commit

A count at scale 6 is `1e6` times the integer the rest of the money path expects. Two families of
site compare or multiply against **unscaled** numbers, and a one-sided change silently breaks both:

**Caps become wrong by 1e6 (every limit bites a million times too early).**

* `crates/busbar-kernel-budget/src/decide.rs` ~331-349 — `cell.total_tokens()` / `total_input()` /
  `total_output()` / `total_cache_read()` compared against `bucket.tokens_cap`,
  `tokens_input_cap`, … which are unscaled operator config.
* `crates/busbar-kernel/src/governance/state.rs` ~1925-1941 — the same fold, same comparison.

**Prices overbill by 1e6.**

* `crates/busbar-kernel-ledger/src/cost/posting.rs:320-322` —
  `u128::from(quantity.amount).saturating_mul(unit_price_nanos)`.
* `crates/busbar-kernel-budget/src/price.rs:113-118` — `reserved_nanos(&BTreeMap<String, u64>)`,
  `u128::from(n).saturating_mul(rate)`.
* `crates/busbar-kernel-ledger/src/cost/rate.rs` (the #44 card-build boundary).

**Do NOT resolve this by dividing the count back down before the multiply.** #44 permits rounding
only on a per-N-units *division* term; a measurement is not a division, and a divide-then-multiply
reintroduces exactly the rounding #81 forbids. The rates move to the same fixed-point scale, and the
multiply becomes `Count::checked_mul_wide` summed once — that is what `checked_mul_wide` is for.

## 3a. THE PREPARED CAPS+RATES CHANGE — written out, NOT landed

> **HELD.** The architect holds this until the crate fold settles. It is written here so landing it
> is mechanical, not a fresh design exercise. Sites measured 2026-09-22.

### The arithmetic, and why it is byte-identical on every count that exists today

A `Count` is `units x 10^6`. A rate is already an integer: `nano_rate(micro_per_unit) =
round(micro_per_unit x 1000)` nanos per WHOLE unit (`kernel-ledger/src/cost/rate.rs:40`, the #44
card-build boundary, unchanged).

* **Today:** `amount_nanos = units x nano_rate`.
* **After:** `amount_nanos = (count.micros() x nano_rate) / 10^6`.

**For a WHOLE count these are the same integer, exactly.** `count.micros() = units x 10^6`, so the
product is divisible by `10^6` with no remainder and the division is not a rounding at all. Every
count in the golden corpus is whole, so **the oracle does not move on any existing data** — which is
what makes this commit landable rather than a signed divergence. A FRACTIONAL count is the only case
that reaches a remainder, and there #44 applies exactly as written: it is a per-N-units DIVISION
term, so banker's rounding, applied ONCE to the accumulated total and never per row
(`Count::checked_mul_wide` exists for precisely that — it hands back the exact wide product so the
sum happens before any rounding decision).

**Caps scale the CAP UP, never the count down.** `tokens >= cap` becomes
`count.micros() >= cap x 10^6`. No division, no rounding, and a cap bites at the same real quantity
it bites at today — which is the assertion the landing commit must carry as a test.

### The exact hunks

**(1) `crates/busbar-kernel-budget/src/decide.rs:376`**
```rust
// before
} else if bucket.tokens_cap.is_some_and(|cap| tokens >= cap) {
// after — the CAP is lifted to the count's scale; the count is never divided down
} else if bucket.tokens_cap.is_some_and(|cap| tokens >= cap.saturating_mul(COUNT_SCALE_FACTOR)) {
```
`bucket.tokens_cap: Option<u64>` (`kernel-budget/src/chain.rs:42`, `:225`; `kernel/src/cost.rs:312`)
is UNSCALED operator config and stays unscaled — operators keep writing `tokens_cap: 100000`.

**(2) `crates/busbar-kernel/src/governance/state.rs:1981`, `:1983`, `:1985`** — the same three
comparisons (`tokens_cap`, `tokens_input_cap`, `tokens_output_cap`), same treatment. These are a
SECOND COPY of the decide.rs fold; they must move together or the two paths disagree about when a
limit bites. (That duplication is itself worth a ruling — see the standing "one ruling, one
implementation" rule.)

**(3) The cell accumulators.** `cell.total_tokens()` / `total_input()` / `total_output()` /
`total_cache_read()` / `total_cache_write()` (`kernel-budget/src/cells.rs:137,156,…` and
`busbar-contract/src/records.rs:702,718,723,729`) return `u64` whole units today and must return the
scale-6 mantissa. Their inputs are `usage_units`, so this lands with step 2 of the order below, not
separately.

**(4) `crates/busbar-kernel-ledger/src/cost/posting.rs:320-322`**
```rust
// before
amount_nanos: u128::from(quantity.amount).saturating_mul(unit_price_nanos),
// after — the exact wide product; the /10^6 happens ONCE on the accumulated total, not per row
amount_nanos_wide: i128::from(quantity.amount).checked_mul(i128::from(unit_price_nanos))?,
```

**(5) `crates/busbar-kernel-budget/src/price.rs:113-118`** — `reserved_nanos(&BTreeMap<String,u64>)`
folds `u128::from(n).saturating_mul(rate)`. Same change: accumulate the wide product, reduce once.
Note it SATURATES today; the replacement must be CHECKED — a saturated money total is a wrong total
that looks like a right one.

**(6) `crates/busbar-kernel-ledger/src/cost/rate.rs:40`** — `nano_rate` is UNCHANGED. It is the #44
card-build boundary, it already produces an integer, and the `no-float-money` gate exempts exactly
that one file. Do not touch it.

### The test the landing commit must carry

A cap configured at N still refuses at exactly N whole units — asserted through BOTH fold sites
(`decide.rs` and `governance/state.rs`), with one fractional case either side of the boundary. If
that test is absent the commit is not landable, because "every limit wrong by 10^6" is precisely the
failure that would otherwise ship silently.

## 4. What happens to counts already on disk (#81a)

**Answer: nothing happens to them. They are never rewritten, and they are never reinterpreted.**

The persisted form is a **per-record scale discriminator**, the pattern this tree has already shipped
three times (`AuditEntry.scheme` in `busbar-kernel-audit/src/legacy/entry.rs:95-120`,
`TaskEventRow.digest_version` in `busbar-a2a/src/taskstore.rs:148-153`, `needs_legacy_usage_wire` in
`plugin-loader/src/legacy_usage.rs:34-40`). One `#[serde(default = "stored_scale_default")] scale`
field per record:

* **ABSENT** ⇒ `SCALE_WHOLE_UNITS` ⇒ the v1.5.5 shape. A row written before the scale existed holds
  whole units, because that is what those rows are.
* **PRESENT and 6** ⇒ `SCALE_MICRO_UNITS` ⇒ the stored integer is the mantissa.
* **Anything else** ⇒ `CountError::UnknownScale`, a refusal. A row from a future this build cannot
  interpret is refused, not guessed at.

`Count::from_stored(value, scale)` is that branch, and reading is **idempotent** — the property the
alternative does not have.

**A RESCALE IS FORBIDDEN.** `crates/api/src/usage_migration.rs:26-33` states the first-boot fold's
crash-safety proof: a crash mid-scan re-runs the whole scan, which is safe *only because re-folding
an already-folded row adds zero*. `× 10^6` is not idempotent — twice gives `10^12`. The protocol that
is safe for the existing fold is unsafe for a rescale, so there is no migration to write. The single
conversion point (`crates/plugin-loader/src/store_adapter.rs:461`, the read-only run-once first-boot
path) keeps working unchanged because it reads through the branch.

**No orphaned balance, and no silently-reinterpreted one.** Every stored count is either on a row
with no discriminator (read as whole units, which is what it is) or on a row that says scale 6.
There is no ambiguous population in the field: the open `usage_units` map is 1.6.0-new (zero hits at
v1.5.5), so no deployed row can be ambiguous about which shape it is.

**One gap is PARKED, not solved (#81a(f)).** A genuinely fractional count bound for a store plugin at
the older payload schema has nowhere to go — `TierTokensDelta`'s four columns are `i64` whole units,
truncating violates #81, and refusing breaks a working deployment on upgrade, which
`crates/busbar/src/root/migration.rs:46-51` (echoed at
`busbar-kernel-ledger/src/migration.rs:61-62`) names as the one outcome a migration may not produce. It
REFUSES pending an owner ruling. It cannot fire today: no provider has been *shown* to emit a
fractional count on the wire.

**Related, older defect, not caused by this work:** `plugin-loader/src/legacy_usage.rs:22-25` drops
any unit key outside the reserved four on the way out to an older-schema store (with a one-time
warning, per its own header). Non-LLM plane counts therefore never reach such a store at all.

## 5. THE RANGE THAT ACTUALLY BINDS

`i128` at scale 6 holds `±1.70141183460469231731687303715884105727e32` in memory
(`count::MAX` / `count::MIN`, asserted in `tests/count_tests.rs`). **That is not the usable range.**
A persisted count column is a signed 64-bit integer, so anything that must survive being written down
stops at `count::MAX_ACROSS_A_64_BIT_COLUMN` — about **9.2e12 whole units**. Quote that number, not
`1.7e32`. `Count::fits_a_64_bit_column()` is the check.

3. **Caps.** `busbar-kernel-budget/src/decide.rs`, `busbar-kernel/src/governance/state.rs` — the

4. **Rates and the multiply.** `kernel-ledger/src/cost/{posting,rate}.rs`,

7. **Delete the two superseded seams** (`usage_count.rs`, `ir/usage.rs`'s `read_count_u64`) and the

## 7a. A BINARY FLOAT IS ALREADY ON THE BILLING CARRIER — reported, not fixed

`busbar_substrate_values::billing::Billing::Duration { seconds: f64 }`
(`crates/busbar-substrate-values/src/billing.rs:54`) is a **binary float carrying a billable
quantity**. It is filled straight from `Value::as_f64` at two live reader sites:

* `crates/busbar-llm-codec/src/gemini/handler.rs` — `audioDurationSeconds` on the transcription read
* `crates/busbar-llm-codec/src/openai_chat/handler.rs` — `{"type":"duration","seconds":…}` (whisper)

Under #77(8) as amended by #81 that is a money-path float, and it is the same class of defect as the
token counts: a duration that cannot be represented exactly cannot be priced exactly. `12.1` seconds
is no more representable as a double than `27.1` tokens is.

**Not fixed here on purpose.** It is a change to a shared carrier TYPE, not a reader fix: every
producer, every consumer and the `Billing` enum move together, and it has oracle exposure wherever a
duration-metered response is in the corpus. It belongs to the conversion wave, beside the count
change, and it should use the same `Count` (seconds are a measurement at a fixed scale exactly as
tokens are).

**Reachability:** the same two routes as the counts — `Billing::Duration` is produced by
`read_response`, consumed at `busbar-llm/src/engine/attempt/buffered.rs:69`, and reaches the ledger
through the same `ledger_and_meter` seam.

## 8. One in-tree claim that is not supported

`crates/busbar-llm-codec/src/usage_count.rs` (module header, "Cohere's real wire responses do exactly
that") and `crates/busbar-llm-codec/src/cohere/tests/tests.rs:1513` ("its actual wire responses carry
them as FLOATS … This is a real Cohere v2 `/v2/chat` response shape") assert a fact about the
PROVIDER'S WIRE.

**Measured: nothing in the tree supports it.** The only float-spelled token counts anywhere under
`crates/busbar-llm-codec/` are inside those two files' own hand-written fixtures
(`cohere/tests/tests.rs`, `cohere/tests/rerank_tests.rs`) — self-authored assertions, not a capture,
a trace or a citation. The checkable claim is the weaker and still-sufficient one: the provider's
published spec types every count as a JSON `number`, which PERMITS a float spelling, so a reader that
cannot read one is a reader that can bill zero. Anyone editing that prose should say that, and should
not restate the wire claim.

Related, and reported as measured by the money-defect wave (NOT re-verified here — take it as a
lead, not a fact): `read_count_u64`'s non-finite branch is not reachable from the wire, because
`sonic-rs` refuses `1e400` at parse time and fails the whole body before any reader runs.
`Count::parse` keeps the guard regardless — it is defence in depth and it IS reachable from non-wire
callers — but no wire coverage should be claimed for it until someone runs it.
