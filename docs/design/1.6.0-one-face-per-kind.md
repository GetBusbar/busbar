# 1.6.0 — ONE FACE HOME PER KIND

**Status: normative plan, measured.** `docs/design/PLUGIN-TREE.md` §1 says, of the kind table's
`The ONE trait` column: *"`Trait` names the single trait in `busbar-contract` that a crate of this
kind implements; implementing a second kind's trait in one crate is a kind fusion and is RED."*
Measured against the tree at `ad8887f2b`, that sentence is **false for all six non-plane kinds**.
This document is the measurement, the one shape decided from it, and the ordered plan that makes
the sentence true.

It is written the way the store kind was already taken apart on `keep-store-kind-on-contract`
(STORE-4), because that branch is the only place in the tree where a kind's faces have been
separated on purpose, and the shape it arrived at is the shape every other kind is held to here.

---

Three homes, and they do not agree on anything: `busbar-api` for store/auth/secret,
`busbar-plugin-sdk` for hook/export, `busbar-contract` for the six dead traits. A seventh face
(`busbar_api::RoutingPolicy`) is what the node actually calls for the hook kind, and an eighth
(nothing) is what it calls for export.

**What the measurement does NOT say.** It does not say the dead traits are wrong. `kinds::Store`
carries 22 verbs the 1.5.5 wire has no spelling for; `kinds::Hook` carries the seat/declaration
vocabulary the SDK trait has no spelling for. They are the faces the NODE was designed to name.
What is missing is the one translation that joins them to what a plugin ships.

### 1.1 STORE-4 is the reference, and it is the only branch that has drawn the line

`origin/keep-store-kind-on-contract` (`4fc6ce3de`, 63 commits on `468bad131`) separates the store
kind into exactly three parts:

* **the kind face** — `busbar_contract::kinds::Store`, completed 22 → 45 verbs. This is what core,
  the root and the units name. Nothing else.
* **the wire face** — `busbar_contract::store::Store`, 33 verbs, MOVED BY IDENTITY out of
  `busbar_api::Store` (`crates/{api => busbar-contract}/src/store.rs`), with the `busbar-api` block
  DELETED in the same landing. This is what a plugin implements, and it lives in the one crate a
  plugin manifest may name.
* **one translation** — `plugin_loader::store_face::StoreFace` (930 lines), the ONLY place in the
  tree that knows both vocabularies: 24 verbs DELEGATE, 7 TRANSLATE, 14 SHIM.

The envelope PODs (`busbar_plugin::cold::{StoreRequest,StoreResponse}`) did **not** move. The
face's DATA types (`VirtualKey`, `AuditRecord`, `UsageLedger`, the metering rows) DID, because the
face names them and the contract is the crate the face lives in.

That is the shape. Stated as a rule:

> **THE SHAPE.** A kind has exactly two faces and one translation.
> The **kind face** is the trait in `busbar_contract::kinds` — what core, the root and the units
> name, and the only face above the loader.
> The **wire face** is a trait in `busbar_contract::<kind>` — what a plugin implements, named by
> the one crate a plugin manifest may name, together with the data types the face itself names.
> The **envelope** (`busbar_plugin::cold::<kind>`) is the JSON the two halves of the ABI exchange,
> and it stays where it is; `busbar-plugin-sdk` keeps the C glue and the op-dispatch match and
> nothing else.
> Exactly ONE adapter in `busbar-plugin-loader` implements the kind face over the wire face.
> There is no other home. A face outside these two crates is a finding.

the SDK name `busbar_contract::`, and `plugin-sdk` takes the `busbar-contract` dependency F1 also
gives it; the kind-isolation grant is written the way F1's `c969b7280` writes
`secret -> contract` "as it grants `store -> contract`".

One thing is NOT taken from F1: the declared-raise FORM. F1 (`6dd6bfddb`) makes a raise an array of

## 2. THE DECISION — one shape, all six kinds

Applying §1.1 to the measurement of §1, per kind, in the order the plan lands them:

| # | Kind | The MOVE | Legacy home DELETED in the same commit | The contract's dead trait becomes | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **hook** | `busbar_plugin_sdk::HookHandler` → `busbar_contract::hook::HookHandler`; the data types it names (`Route`, `RouteAuth`, `RouteMethod`, `HttpEndpointRequest`, `HttpEndpointResponse`) → `busbar_contract::http_endpoint` | the `plugin-sdk` trait block; `busbar_plugin::cold::http_endpoint` | the **kind face**: `kinds::Hook` is what the node names once `DlopenPolicy` is re-pointed onto it | ~380 moved lines, 12 reader crates re-pointed |
| 2 | **export** | `busbar_plugin_sdk::ExportHandler` → `busbar_contract::export::ExportHandler`; the vocabulary it names (`ExportStream`, `ExportField`) → `busbar_contract::export` | the `plugin-sdk` trait block; the `ExportStream`/`ExportField` half of `busbar_plugin::cold::export` | the **kind face**: `kinds::Export` + `kinds::Anchor` | ~420 moved lines, 6 reader crates re-pointed |
| 3 | **store** | STORE-4b — see §4 | `busbar_api::Store` and its record block | already completed to 45 verbs by STORE-4 | 63 commits, 348 files |
| 4 | **secret** | `busbar_api::SecretModule` → `busbar_contract::secret::SecretModule` (the `SecretError` taxonomy goes with it, folded into the one `PluginError` STORE-4 lands) | the `busbar-api` secret block | the **kind face**: `kinds::Secret`, 6 verbs, adapter in the loader | ~240 moved lines, 4 reader crates |
| 5 | **auth** | `busbar_api::{AuthModule, LoginModule}` → `busbar_contract::auth::{AuthModule, LoginModule}` with the login vocabulary (`BeginLogin`, `LoginForm`, `LoginHop`, `LoginOutcome`, …) | the `busbar-api` auth block | the **kind face**: `kinds::AuthScheme`, 4 verbs. The login half has NO kind-face counterpart today and the plan's last step is the ruling that gives it one or strikes it | ~340 moved lines, 5 reader crates |
| 6 | **egress-auth** | **nothing to move.** The kind has no implemented face, no wire POD, no loader adapter and no plugin | — | `kinds::EgressAuthScheme` is the only face the kind will ever have; it stays, and the gate's zero-implementor row for it stands until the first egress-auth plugin ships | 0 |

**Why the contract's dead trait is kept and not deleted, for five of the six.** The owner rule is
`delete legacy, never build beside`. A trait with zero implementors looks like legacy. It is not:
for store, hook, export, secret and auth the kind trait is the face the node was designed to name,
and STORE-4 proves the shape works by COMPLETING `kinds::Store` rather than striking it. The trait
is deleted only where the kind has no node-side face to name — and measured today, that is
**none of the six**: even egress-auth's trait is the face the egress-auth unit will call. What is
deleted is every DUPLICATE home: `busbar-api`'s three blocks and `busbar-plugin-sdk`'s two.

**Why the wire face goes to `busbar-contract` and not to `busbar-plugin` beside its envelope.**
Measured: `busbar-plugin` already depends on `busbar-api` (`crates/busbar-plugin/Cargo.toml:16`),
so the POD crate already depends on the face crate, and STORE-4 grants `plugin-abi -> contract` for
exactly this reason. The reverse — the contract depending on the POD crate — would put
`busbar-api` under `busbar-contract` transitively, which is the crate being retired, and
`busbar-contract`'s own dependency policy (its `Cargo.toml` preamble) forbids it. The direction is
already decided by the tree; this plan only follows it.

## 3. THE ORDERED PLAN

Each step is one landing: the MOVE, the legacy home deleted in the same commit, the plugin crates
re-pointed, the cells byte-identical, RAIL 13 green.

1. **hook, the PODs** — `busbar_plugin::cold::http_endpoint` → `busbar_contract::http_endpoint`,
   MOVE BY IDENTITY. `busbar-plugin` takes a `busbar-contract` dependency; the
   `plugin-abi -> contract` grant is written in the same commit. Twelve reader crates re-pointed
   (`busbar-core`, `busbar-a2a`, `busbar-llm`, `busbar-mcp`, `busbar-voice`, `busbar-substrate`,
   `busbar`, `plugin-loader`, `plugin-sdk`, `busbar-plugin`, `xtask`, and the two integration
   batteries). No POD field changes → **ABI unchanged**.
2. **hook, the face** — `busbar_plugin_sdk::HookHandler` → `busbar_contract::hook::HookHandler`,
   MOVE BY IDENTITY. `plugin-sdk` keeps `hook_abi_version`, `dispatch_hook`, `hook_dispatch`,
   `HookHandle` — the C glue and the op-dispatch match — and re-points at the contract.
   `busbar-hook-test-plugin` names `busbar_contract::hook::HookHandler`.
3. **export, the PODs and the face** — `ExportStream`/`ExportField` →
   `busbar_contract::export`, `busbar_plugin_sdk::ExportHandler` → `busbar_contract::export`,
   both MOVE BY IDENTITY. `busbar_plugin::cold::export` keeps `EXPORT_ABI_VERSION`,
   `ExportRequest`, `ExportResponse`. `busbar-export-example-plugin` re-pointed.
   No wire token changes → **ABI unchanged**.
4. **the gate** — `construction:one-face-home` (§5), pinned at its measured count, with this
   document's §2 table as its drain schedule; `PLUGIN-TREE.md` §1 rewritten to what is true.
5. **STORE-4b** (§4) — STORE-4 re-cut onto the tip.
6. **secret** — the `busbar-api` secret block → `busbar_contract::secret`, legacy deleted, the
   loader's `DynSecret` re-pointed, `kinds::Secret` given its adapter.
7. **auth** — the `busbar-api` auth block → `busbar_contract::auth`, legacy deleted, `DynAuth`
   re-pointed, `kinds::AuthScheme` given its adapter. `busbar-api` has nothing left after this
   step and the crate is struck.
8. **egress-auth** — the row that stays red until a plugin of the kind exists.

Steps 1–4 land on `keep-one-face-home-per-kind`. Steps 5–8 are owed.

## 5. THE GATE

`construction:one-face-home`. Two claims over one measurement, because they are the two ways the
same rule is broken:

* **a contract kind trait with zero implementors in the workspace** — the trait names a face
  nothing implements, so the kind's real face is somewhere else and `PLUGIN-TREE.md` §1 is false
  for that row;
* **a plugin implementing a kind face outside the one home** — an `impl <face> for` in a plugin
  crate naming a trait that lives anywhere but `busbar-contract`.

It is a construction row and not an extension of `kind-isolation:faces` by measurement:
`kind_isolation.rs:165`'s `ROW_FACES` counts *a crate implementing ANOTHER KIND's entry face* —
`[[face]] crate/face/count` rows for `busbar-plane-admin`/`Plane`, `busbar-a2a`/`Transport`,
`busbar-plugin-loader`/`Store`, `busbar`/`Store`. That rule reads the CRATE axis (is this crate
what it says it is); this one reads the FACE axis (does the face live where the design says). They
share no input and would share no detail line.

Pinned at its measured count on the commit that lands it, with §2's table as the drain schedule:
each row of §2 is one step of the drain, and the count falls by exactly the faces that step moves.
The count reaches zero when step 8's row is the only one left, and that row falls when the first
egress-auth plugin ships.

## 6. WHAT `PLUGIN-TREE.md` §1 SAYS AFTER THIS

The `The ONE trait` column names two things per kind, because there are two faces and the document
was written as if there were one:

* **the kind face** — the `busbar_contract::kinds` trait core names;
* **the wire face** — the `busbar_contract::<kind>` trait a plugin implements.

A crate of a kind implements the **wire face** and nothing else; the **kind face** is implemented
exactly once, by the loader's adapter for that kind, and the loader is not a kind. That is the
sentence §1 can carry truthfully, and it is what `construction:one-face-home` measures.
