# `cut` — what happens when a budget runs out mid-stream

Status: 1.6.0 design + owner ruling — rescued from `keep-money-d-3`; needs owner confirmation of as-built state on the integration line.

`on_exhaust: cut` is the third exhaustion word. `block` refuses the unit before it starts and
`downgrade` sends it somewhere cheaper; `cut` admits it, meters it as it flows, and STOPS IT at the
cap. Nothing in the tree runs a cut today: the boot check added in `276f7ab6a` refuses a config that
spells `cut`, so the word is closed in the config grammar and unreachable at runtime. This document
is the design that removes that refusal, and it records the OWNER'S RULING on the two questions the
implementing agent could not answer for itself.

## The ruling

**A cut IS a reason the client is told.** Where the protocol carries a place to say so, a cut ends
the stream WITH AN ERROR FRAME naming budget exhaustion — not a silent end of stream. A client whose
answer stops halfway and whose transport says "that's all" cannot tell a completed answer from a
truncated one, and the difference is the whole content of the event: one is the model finishing, the
other is the operator's budget finishing. Silence would make every cut look like a short answer, and
the first support ticket is "the model got lazy" rather than "your budget ran out at 10:14".

So the cut takes ONE `ReasonCode` and ONE `RefusalReason`, welded by the `reasons!` macro in
`crates/busbar-caps/src/decision.rs` exactly as that macro intends: the kernel decides in
`ReasonCode`, the client is told in `RefusalReason`, and the macro's third column is the join that
makes a reason with no client-side spelling fail to compile.

**The 122 exhaustive match sites are a smell, and they are fixed first.** Adding a reason today
means editing `crates/busbar-plane-admin/src/refusal.rs` (84 sites), `crates/busbar-plane-llm/src/codec.rs`
(38), and the a2a, mcp, voice and transport renderers besides. Every one of those matches is
answering the SAME question — what status class and what word does this reason render as — and
answering it separately is how two transports come to call one refusal by two names. The
de-duplication is a change of its own, and it comes BEFORE the cut reason: adding a row to a table
is a reviewable one-line change, while adding a 123rd arm to a pile of matches is a change nobody
can read.

## The shape

### 1. One reason-rendering table, in the contract (`[SEAM]`, first)

The contract owns `RefusalReason` (`crates/busbar-contract/src/unit.rs`), so the contract owns the
DATA that says what each reason renders as: a table of `reason -> (status class, word)`. Every plane
and every transport renders GENERICALLY from that table. The 84-arm match becomes a lookup; so does
the 38-arm one.

* **A reason absent from the table is a refusal, never a wildcard arm.** The table is total over
  `RefusalReason` — either by construction (an array indexed by the enum, walked by a test over the
  full vocabulary) or by a boot check that walks every variant and fails to start if one is missing.
  A wildcard `_ =>` arm is the exact defect being removed: it renders an unknown reason as the
  nearest neighbour and nothing reds.
* **Every existing refusal envelope stays byte-identical.** The existing cells for those 122 sites
  ARE the proof: the table is populated from what each match arm says today, and if a single envelope
  moves, the table is wrong, not the cell. No cell may be edited to match a new rendering.
* Red-first: a reason planted into the vocabulary and left out of the table must red — at compile
  time where the totality is structural, at boot where it is checked.

### 2. The cut reason (one table row, one arm)

With the table in place the cut is: one `reasons!` row (`BudgetCut => "budget_cut", BudgetCut`), one
`RefusalReason` variant, one table row giving its status class and word. Nothing else in any plane or
transport changes.

**No new `Abort` shape.** `Abort` (`crates/busbar-caps/src/unit_end.rs`) says "one encoding, not
two", and it is right: `Abort::Kernel { reason }` already carries a reason, and a cut is a reason. The
earlier proposal — a cut-shaped `Abort` arm carrying the class, the cap and the delivered quantity —
is REJECTED under this ruling, because those three facts are not the ending, they are the METERING,
and the metering already has a home: the ledger's facts line for the unit carries the class, the
metered quantity and the outcome. Putting them on `Abort` too would be the second encoding the type's
own doc comment refuses.

### 3. The sever

`FirstByteBody::poll_next` in `crates/busbar-llm/src/engine/response_body.rs` is the only place a
streaming unit's quantity accrues per chunk (`StreamTranslate` accrual against the `UsageSink`
admission handle). The meter step in the unit crates runs ONCE at unit end and cannot stop anything
mid-body — the earlier brief named it and was wrong.

At the cap, the body:

1. stops pulling from upstream,
2. emits the transport's error frame for `BudgetCut` where the protocol has one (SSE: a terminal
   `error` event before `[DONE]`; a framed transport: its own error frame), and ends the stream,
3. releases the hold and posts the unit with outcome `cut` and the DELIVERED quantity — what
   actually flowed, never the cap and never the estimate.

Red-first, against a fake streaming upstream: the stream ends at the cap, the frame carries the cut
reason, the ledger line reads `cut` with the delivered quantity, the hold is released. The `block`
and `downgrade` twins must stay BYTE-IDENTICAL to today.

### 4. Only then, the boot refusal narrows

`276f7ab6a` refuses `cut` at boot for every class. Once the sever exists, that refusal narrows to the
classes the meter cannot count mid-stream: a class whose quantity is knowable only at unit end cannot
be cut mid-body, and a non-streaming unit cannot be cut mid-body at all. For those, `cut` stays a BOOT
refusal — an operator who asks for an enforcement this tree cannot perform is told so at boot, not
silently given a `block`.

## Alternatives, and why not

**Silent end-of-stream, no reason (the previous agent's proposal).** Cheap: no reason vocabulary
change, no renderer edits, and it dodges the "no refusal envelope after bytes have flowed" problem
that a mid-stream REFUSAL would have. Rejected by the owner: it makes a cut indistinguishable from a
short answer, which is precisely the fact the operator turned the feature on to produce. The
objection it was built on — that a refusal ENVELOPE cannot be sent after bytes flowed — is real but
narrower than it looked: the answer is the protocol's ERROR FRAME, which every streaming dialect in
this tree has, not the pre-stream refusal envelope.

**A cut-shaped `Abort` arm with no `ReasonCode`.** Keeps the refusal vocabulary untouched and so
touches no renderer. Rejected: it puts an ending in the journal that no reason names, which is the
two-spellings defect `Abort`'s own doc comment describes, and it leaves the client told nothing.

**Add the 123rd match arm in each of the six crates.** The mechanical reading of "one ReasonCode +
one RefusalReason". Rejected as the smell it is: the cost of a reason should be a row, and 122
duplicated answers to one question is a defect that a cut would merely make worse.

**Waive the cut and ship `block` semantics under the `cut` word.** Rejected outright: an enforcement
the tree does not perform, spelled as one it does, is the silent misenforcement `276f7ab6a` exists to
prevent.

## Order of work

1. `[SEAM]` the contract-side reason-rendering table; every plane and transport renders from it; all
   existing envelopes byte-identical; a reason missing from the table reds.
2. The `BudgetCut` reason: one `reasons!` row, one `RefusalReason` variant, one table row.
3. The sever at `FirstByteBody::poll_next`, red-first against a fake streaming upstream.
4. Narrow the boot refusal from every class to the uncountable ones.

Each step is separable and each is red before it is green. Step 1 lands on its own merits whether or
not `cut` ever ships.
